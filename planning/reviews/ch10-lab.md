# Review of ch10-lab

## Errors

1. `docs/chapter10_offline_rl/lab.md` — "the mean $\max_a Q(s,a)$ curve exposes the overestimation" / "the naive DQN's mean $\max_a Q$ on held-out states ends at 34.7 versus CQL's 26.9, drifting upward late in training" — the two printed numbers do **not** show overestimation; they show large *under*estimation, and the cause is a training-setup defect that the writer already diagnosed for FQE but left in place here. Scale check: the greedy expert's true discounted value on CartPole-v1 with $\gamma=0.99$ is $\sum_{t=0}^{499}0.99^t = 99.34$ (I simulated the frozen `expert_q.pt` for 20 episodes: mean discounted return 99.34, mean episode length 500.0), and the lab's own "truth" for the smoothed target is 93.03. The reported max-Q endpoints are 34.75 (naive DQN) and 26.88 (CQL) — about 1/3 of the true scale, i.e. **under**-estimates. Root cause: `train_offline.py::train_q_offline` syncs the target network every 500 of 15,000 gradient steps, so the Bellman backup propagates only ~30 steps and the value it can represent is at most $(1-0.99^{30})/(1-0.99) = 26.0$ — exactly the failure the writer diagnosed for FQE ("$(1-\gamma^{10})/(1-\gamma)\approx 9.6$, exactly the observed value"). Re-running the shipped code on the expert dataset with `target_sync_every=100` (the fix the writer applied to FQE) gives: naive DQN mean max-Q = **1,466,013** (runaway OOD overestimation) vs CQL = **81.4** (bounded, true scale 99.34); the greedy returns are unchanged (9.4 vs 500.0). So the divergence the page wants to demonstrate only appears once the target is synced often enough, and the shipped configuration masks it. Suggested fix: sync the target every 100 steps in `train_q_offline` (or expose `--target-sync-every`), re-run the reference numbers, and rewrite the paragraph around the new (much larger) gap; if the 500-step sync is kept, the text must say the curve measures under-propagation, not overestimation.

2. `docs/chapter10_offline_rl/lab.md` — "**CQL is the only method that is never bad.** It matches or beats both baselines on all four datasets (242–500)." — contradicted by the page's own table: on the random dataset CQL scores 241.6 while the naive DQN scores 332.9, so CQL does *not* match the naive DQN there (the very next sentence admits "the penalty costs a little on random data (241.6 vs naive DQN's luckier 332.9)"). The identical claim appears in `code/chapter10_offline_rl/README.md` ("CQL matches or beats every baseline on every dataset"). Suggested fix: "CQL is the only method that is never *catastrophic*: it matches or beats both baselines on medium/expert/mixed, and loses only on random data (241.6 vs 332.9)".

3. `docs/chapter10_offline_rl/lab.md` — "FQE lands at 86–92 from _every_ dataset ... it undershoots truth by ~7 points everywhere" (and `README.md`: "FQE stays within ~7 points of truth on _all four_ datasets") — the random dataset's FQE is 92.23 against a truth of 93.03, an undershoot of **0.8 points**, not ~7; only medium (86.46), expert (86.34) and mixed (85.69) undershoot by 6.6–7.3. The same sentence's "86–92" also excludes 92.23. Suggested fix: quote the per-dataset gaps (0.8 / 6.6 / 6.7 / 7.3 points) instead of a single "~7 points everywhere", or restrict the claim to the three non-random datasets.

4. `docs/chapter10_offline_rl/lab.md` and `code/chapter10_offline_rl/README.md` — "the naive DQN's mean $\max_a Q$ **on held-out states**" — they are not held out. `train_offline.py` sets `eval_states = torch.as_tensor(arrays["states"][:n_eval_states])` with `n_eval_states = min(2000, len(arrays["states"]))`, i.e. the first 2000 transitions of the *training* dataset, while training samples uniformly from the whole array; the plot's y-label ("mean max_a Q(s,a) on held-out states") and the README repeat the same claim. A reader will conclude the curve measures generalization to unseen states, which it does not. Suggested fix: say "on the first 2000 dataset states" (or actually hold out a split) and rename the axis label accordingly.

5. `docs/chapter10_offline_rl/lab.md` — "on random data it scores 9.1, faithfully copying noise" and "Why behaviour cloning copies the behaviour policy (including denoising it)" — the copy *is* faithful, but the score is not what the sentence claims. I retrained the BC net on the random dataset: its softmax is essentially uniform (mean entropy 0.6922 nats vs the maximum $\log 2 = 0.6931$; agreement with the dataset actions 0.504), i.e. BC did reproduce the uniform behaviour policy. What breaks the score is the *evaluation*: `evaluate_greedy` takes the argmax of the logits, turning the copied uniform distribution into a deterministic policy (97.4 % of the 2000 first states map to action 1), which falls over in ~9 steps → 9.1, *below* the behaviour's own 21.9. So BC on random data does not "score 9.1 by copying noise"; it scores 9.1 because greedy evaluation of a faithfully copied stochastic policy is a different (much worse) policy. This also contradicts the lab's framing that BC "copies the behaviour policy" — on random data it is strictly worse than the behaviour it copied. Suggested fix: state that BC reproduces the behaviour *distribution*, that argmax evaluation then denoises it into a deterministic policy, and that on a uniform-random dataset that deterministic policy is worse than the behaviour (9.1 vs 21.9).

6. `code/chapter10_offline_rl/ope.py` (and the OPE table in `docs/chapter10_offline_rl/lab.md`) — `train_fqe` backs up with `pi_next = q_target_policy(ns).argmax(dim=1, keepdim=True)`, i.e. the **greedy** expert, while the "truth" (`true_target_value`) and the importance ratios (`target_probs_for`, `TARGET_EPS = 0.05`) all refer to the **5 %-smoothed** expert. The two policies have different values: I measured the greedy expert at 99.34 and the smoothed target at 93.03 (the lab's own truth), so the FQE column estimates $V^{\text{greedy}}$ but is compared against $V^{\text{smoothed}}$ — a built-in 6.3-point (≈7 %) offset that is exactly the "FQE undershoots truth by ~7 points" pattern the page reports. Suggested fix: either draw the FQE backup action from the smoothed target distribution (matching `TARGET_EPS`) or compare the FQE column against the greedy policy's online value (99.34), in which case FQE is 7–14 % low rather than 0.8–7 % low.

7. `docs/chapter10_offline_rl/lab.md` (Exercise 4 hint) — "_Hint: longer horizons multiply more ratios into each weight; check the ESS column._" — the hint is wrong on both counts. (a) The number of ratios in a trajectory weight is the episode length (fixed by CartPole's 500-step cap), not the horizon; changing $\gamma$ does not add ratios to any weight. (b) The ESS does not depend on $\gamma$ at all — the weights $w_i=\prod_t\rho_t$ contain no discount factor. I recomputed the random and expert datasets at $\gamma=0.9,0.99,0.999$: ESS = 1.0773 (random) and 5.2512 (expert) at *all three* $\gamma$ values, bit-identical. What actually changes is the scale of the estimand: the true online value of the target is 9.95 ($\gamma=0.9$), 93.03 ($\gamma=0.99$) and 347.0 ($\gamma=0.999$), so every estimator moves by that factor; in relative terms PDIS degrades most (expert data: PDIS 9.56 vs truth 9.95 at $\gamma=0.9$; 78.84 vs 93.03 at $\gamma=0.99$; 274.4 vs 347.0 at $\gamma=0.999$), while WIS tracks the truth (9.98 / 90.91 / 347.7) and FQE keeps its bias. Suggested fix: replace the hint with "the estimand's scale changes with $\gamma$ (about 10× smaller at 0.9, 4× larger at 0.999), so all three estimators move together; the ESS column is unaffected because the weights contain no discount factor — check which estimator's *relative* error grows with the effective horizon".

8. `docs/chapter10_offline_rl/lab.md` (Exercise 3 hint) — "_Hint: what happens to $\rho = \pi_e/\pi_b$ when $\pi_b$ puts zero mass where $\pi_e$ acts?_" — the premise is inverted for this dataset: the random behaviour is uniform, so $\pi_b$ has full support and never puts zero mass anywhere. The degeneracy comes from the *target*: with `TARGET_EPS = 0.0` the greedy target assigns probability 0 to the non-greedy action, so $\rho \in \{0, 2\}$. I ran this on the random dataset: every episode contains at least one step where the behaviour picked the zero-probability action, so **all** 2287 trajectory weights are exactly 0, the WIS denominator is 0 and `wis_estimate` returns **NaN**, and ESS = 0. PDIS does *not* collapse (28.91), because a per-decision product only needs a nonzero prefix. Suggested fix: "with a fully greedy target the ratios are 0 or 2; since each episode contains at least one zero-ratio step, every trajectory weight is 0, WIS becomes 0/0 = NaN and the ESS collapses to 0, while PDIS stays finite because it only needs a nonzero prefix product".

9. `docs/chapter10_offline_rl/lab.md` (Exercise 5) — "Fine-tune the CQL-on-random policy with 20 online DQN episodes ... _Hint: reuse `expert_q.pt` as the Q initialization and keep epsilon low._" — the exercise is not executable as written: `train_offline.py` never saves the trained networks (it writes only `offline/summary.json`, `offline/summary.csv` and the two PNGs), so there is no "CQL-on-random policy" to fine-tune; and `expert_q.pt` is the *collection-time online DQN*, not the CQL policy — initialising from it would also make the "compare against 20 episodes from scratch" comparison meaningless, since the "from scratch" arm would secretly start from the same network. Suggested fix: have `train_offline.py` save each trained Q-network (e.g. `offline/<dataset>_<method>_q.pt`) and let the exercise initialise from `offline/random_cql_q.pt`; or rewrite the exercise around the expert dataset with `expert_q.pt` explicitly named as the starting point for *both* arms.

10. `docs/chapter10_offline_rl/lab.md` (Exercise 2) — "Collect with `--num-transitions 5000` and compare naive DQN on the small vs full expert data. _Hint: fewer transitions means thinner coverage, so the collapse should arrive earlier ..._" — two problems. (a) The command is not runnable as an isolated comparison: `collect_datasets.py --num-transitions 5000` rewrites **all four** datasets (and retrains the online DQN, hence a different expert) in the default `runs/chapter10_offline_rl/`, destroying the "full expert data" baseline the exercise asks to compare against; the exercise never mentions `--outdir`. (b) The hint's prediction is not meaningful: the collapse on the full expert dataset is already total (9.3 out of 500), so it cannot "arrive earlier"; only the second half of the hint (the max-Q curve separating from CQL's) is a testable prediction. Suggested fix: "collect with `--num-transitions 5000 --outdir runs/ch10_small` and compare the naive-DQN max-Q curve against the full-data run", and drop the "collapse arrives earlier" claim.

11. `docs/.vitepress/config.mjs` / `docs/chapter10_offline_rl/lab.md` — the new page is not registered in the sidebar, and its label does not exist in the book's numbering. The Chapter 10 block still lists only 10.1–10.3, so "10.L Lab: Offline RL from Fixed Datasets" is unreachable from the site navigation and is omitted from the PDF/EPUB builds (the same defect was flagged for `7.R` in the ch07 review). In addition, every other entry in `config.mjs` is numbered `N.M` (and the appendices use `A.1`, `B.1`, …), while this page uses `10.L`; `scripts/fix-heading-numbers.mjs` matches sidebar entries with the regex `text:\s*'(\d+)\.(\d+)\s…'`, so it can never sync a `10.L` heading — and once a numeric entry (e.g. `10.4`) is added, the script rewrites the body H1 to `# 10.4 …` while the frontmatter `title: '10.L Lab: Offline RL from Fixed Datasets'` is left stale, so the nav title and the page title would disagree. Suggested fix: add the entry to the Chapter 10 sidebar block as `text: '10.4 Hands-on: Offline RL from Fixed Datasets', link: '/chapter10_offline_rl/lab'`, rename the H1 to `# 10.4 Hands-on: Offline RL from Fixed Datasets`, and set the frontmatter title to the same string.

12. `docs/chapter10_offline_rl/lab.md` / `code/chapter10_offline_rl/README.md` — the quoted diagnostic "mean max-Q on held-out states ends at 34.7 vs CQL's 26.9" is not stable and is not reproduced by the documented per-dataset command: the Q-network's initialization depends on the *global* torch RNG, which is consumed by the networks of the previously processed datasets and by the throw-away `QMLP` that `bc_as_q` constructs. With the documented full run (`--dataset all`) I measure 34.75 for the expert panel, but running the documented `train_offline.py --dataset expert --seed 0 --device cpu` gives **37.73** for the same configuration, and a third initialization (same script, BC network trained first but without the `bc_as_q` step) gives 66.64. The qualitative ordering survives, but a diagnostic quoted to one decimal as evidence for a mechanism needs to be stable or the page must say which command produced it. Suggested fix: seed each (dataset, method) run from a per-run seed (e.g. `seed + hash(dataset)`) so the numbers do not depend on the order of the datasets, and quote the resulting values.

## Doubts

1. `docs/chapter10_offline_rl/lab.md` — "From the random dataset, both PDIS (27.71) and WIS (18.37) collapse onto the behaviour value (19.24)" — only WIS is close to the behaviour value; PDIS is 44 % above it (27.71 vs 19.24). PDIS is unbiased in expectation, so 27.71 is best read as one high-variance realisation rather than "collapsing onto" the behaviour value. This weakens the sentence slightly but not the conclusion.

2. `docs/chapter10_offline_rl/lab.md` — "**Off-policy evaluation** of the expert target (true discounted value from 50 online rollouts: 93.03; $\gamma = 0.99$; dataset MC is the plain behaviour average)" — `dataset_mc` is the *γ-discounted* behaviour average (19.24 for the random dataset, whose undiscounted episode return is 21.9), not a plain average. Since the first table labels the undiscounted episode return as "behaviour", the two tables mix conventions under similar labels; worth stating "discounted behaviour average".

3. `docs/chapter10_offline_rl/lab.md` (Step 3) — "FQE regresses $y = r + \gamma Q_{\bar{}}(s', \pi_e(s'))$ with no `max` and no ratios, then averages over initial states" — the averaging is over the *dataset's* episode-initial states (`arrays["states"][e[0]]` for each dataset episode), not over the environment's reset distribution. For CartPole these coincide (every episode starts from `env.reset()`), so the estimate is fine, but the text should say which initial states are used.

4. `docs/chapter10_offline_rl/lab.md` (Step 1) — "the best checkpoint seen during training is kept (online evaluation during online training is legitimate …)" — selecting the best of 20 five-episode greedy evaluations (every 25 episodes) is a maximum over noisy estimates, so the "expert" is optimistically biased by construction; the reported "online DQN greedy return: 500.0" is that selected maximum rather than an unbiased estimate. A one-line disclosure (or averaging the last few checkpoints) would be cleaner.

5. `docs/chapter10_offline_rl/lab.md` — "**CQL is the only method that is never bad**" — even after fixing finding 2, CQL's 241.6 on random data is the third-worst cell in the table (only BC on random data, 9.1, and the naive DQN on expert data, 9.3, are worse), so "never bad" overstates the result; the honest summary is "CQL is never catastrophic, but it is not free either".

6. `code/chapter10_offline_rl/train_offline.py` — in smoke mode (`--steps 400`) the target network is never synced (`target_sync_every = 500`), so the smoke run exercises a learner whose Bellman target is the initial random network. The smoke test still passes (it only checks that the artifacts exist), but the "smoke" configuration does not test the algorithm at all. A smaller sync interval in smoke mode (or `--target-sync-every` derived from `--steps`) would make the smoke run meaningful.

7. `planning/reports/ch10-lab.md` — "FQE underestimated by ~10x (9.3 vs truth ~93) … $(1-\gamma^{10})/(1-\gamma) \approx 9.6$ — exactly the observed value" — the derivation is right ((1−0.99¹⁰)/0.01 = 9.56) and I reproduced every published number of the reference run exactly (collect: 21.9/203.2/490.9/233.3 with 2286/244/101/214 episodes; train: 9.1/332.9/241.6, 500/479.5/500, 500/9.3/500, 500/500/500; OPE: truth 93.03 ± 19.31 and the four rows of the table). The concern is only that the same diagnosis was applied to FQE but not to the DQN/CQL trainers (finding 1), and that the report's "Needs external verification: none on factual claims" should mention the target-sync dependence of the quoted max-Q endpoints.

## Verdict

The lab is unusually well executed: every number in the page and in the README reproduces exactly from the shipped code (I re-ran the whole pipeline: collection, offline training and OPE), the unit tests' hand computations check out, the CQL formula matches Section 10.1, and there are no fabricated citations. The most important problem is that the page's headline diagnostic is misread: the max-Q curve is quoted as evidence of "overestimation" when 34.75 and 26.88 are large *under*-estimates relative to the true scale of 99.34, because `train_q_offline` still uses the 500-step target sync whose failure the writer themselves diagnosed for FQE — with the sync at 100 the naive DQN's Q explodes to 1.5e6 while CQL stays at 81.4, which is the demonstration the page promises. Second, the FQE column is compared against the wrong reference policy (greedy backup vs smoothed truth, 99.34 vs 93.03), which manufactures the "FQE undershoots by ~7 points" story, and the two summary claims "CQL matches or beats both baselines on all four datasets" and "FQE undershoots truth by ~7 points everywhere" are both contradicted by the page's own table. Third, four of the six extension exercises contain technically wrong or non-executable instructions (γ/ESS, zero-mass attribution, the missing CQL checkpoint, the destructive collection command), and the page is not in the sidebar under a label that matches the book's numbering. Fix the target-sync setting, re-run the reference numbers, correct the three contradicted claims and the exercise hints, and register the page as 10.4.
