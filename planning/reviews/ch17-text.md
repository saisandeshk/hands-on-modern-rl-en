## Errors

1. `docs/chapter17_prm_search/discriminative-prm.md:53-54` (repeated at `:261`) — "800K steps × 30 seconds per step = 24,000 hours" / "roughly **12 person-years** of work" — unit error, off by a factor of 3.6. 800,000 × 30 s = 24,000,000 s = **6,667 hours**, and 6,667 h / 2,000 h per person-year = **≈3.3 person-years**, not 24,000 h / 12 person-years (the author converted seconds to "thousands of seconds" and then read them as hours). The same wrong number is repeated in the Section Summary bullet ("800,000 steps is roughly 12 person-years of labeling"). This is pre-existing, but it is inside the page the revision was supposed to verify (the writer's report claims "every in-text numeric example" was recomputed with `.venv/bin/python`), and the estimate is ~3.6× inflated. — Suggested fix: write "800K steps × 30 seconds per step = 6,667 hours ≈ 3.3 person-years" in both places.

2. `docs/chapter17_prm_search/parallel-reasoning-and-summary.md:43` — "After normalization, $-3$ appears 4 times and the majority vote is clear." — miscount. The five answers are `x = -3, x = -2, x = -3.0, x = 3, x = -3`; after normalizing `-3.0 → -3` the tally is −3 ×3, −2 ×1, +3 ×1. The value −3 appears **three** times, not four (the `x = 3` is a different answer and must not be folded into −3). The majority conclusion still holds (3/5), so only the count is wrong, but the sentence is the whole point of the normalization example. — Suggested fix: "After normalization, $-3$ appears 3 times and the majority vote is clear."

3. `docs/chapter17_prm_search/outcome-vs-process.md:242` — "Let a large model first generate a natural-language check ("This step tries to divide both sides by 2, which should give $2k^2=q^2$, but it is written as $k$")" — this is the _old_ retelling of the running $\sqrt{2}$ example and it is factually garbled: the faulty step in the example is $4k=q^2$, not "$k$". The revision rewrote exactly this quote in `generative-prm.md` (line 7 and the example block at lines 46-47) to the standardized form, and rewrote the same example twice on this page (lines 5, 44, 88), but left this fourth retelling untouched. So the chapter now tells the same step in two mutually inconsistent ways, and the stale one is wrong. — Suggested fix: replace with the standardized wording used in 17.3, e.g. "Substituting $p=2k$ gives $4k^2=2q^2$, i.e. $2k^2=q^2$, but the step writes $4k=q^2$".

4. `docs/chapter17_prm_search/review.md` (new file) — the page is **not registered in the sidebar** (`docs/.vitepress/config.mjs` still lists only 17.1–17.6 for Chapter 17), so on the built site the page is unreachable from the navigation; `AGENTS.md` requires every navigable page to be listed there. The writer's own report defers this ("Coordinator to add to sidebar"), which means the branch as delivered has an orphan page. Related: the page number `17.R` does not exist in this book's numbering scheme — every other chapter ends with a plain number (`5.6 Summary and Exercises`, `7.8 Summary and Exercises`) and `scripts/fix-heading-numbers.mjs` only understands `X.Y` sidebar entries (`/text:\s*'(\d+)\.(\d+)\s([^']*)'/`), so `17.R` can never be synced or checked by the tooling. — Suggested fix: add `{ text: '17.7 Summary and Exercises', link: '/chapter17_prm_search/review' }` to the Chapter 17 sidebar block and change the front-matter `title:` and the H1 to `17.7 Summary and Exercises`.

5. `docs/chapter17_prm_search/review.md`, Exercise 7 solution — "`product` ties with `{'A': 0.1296, 'B': 0.1296}` (either key may be returned on the tie)" — false for the code given. `max(scored, key=...)` returns the **first** maximal element, and `cands` is ordered `A, B`, so `select` deterministically returns `('A', {'A': 0.1296, 'B': 0.1296})`. Verified by running the snippet: `product ('A', {'A': 0.1296, 'B': 0.1296})`. — Suggested fix: say the tie is broken deterministically in favour of `A` (insertion order), or make the tie-break explicit in the code (e.g. `max(..., key=lambda k: (scored[k], k == 'A'))`).

6. `docs/chapter17_prm_search/review.md`, Exercise 8 solution — "the bonus falls to about $1.414\sqrt{4.700/15} \approx 0.791$ and UCB to about $1.391$" / "Expected output (verified)" — the numbers are off in the last digit: $1.41421356\sqrt{4.700480365792417/15} = 0.791533$ (rounds to 0.792, not 0.791) and UCB $= 1.391533$ (the code's own `round(..., 3)` prints **1.392**, not 1.391). Everything else in that exercise checks out exactly. — Suggested fix: write 0.792 and 1.392 (or print the values with more precision).

## Doubts

1. `docs/chapter17_prm_search/generative-prm.md` — "Relative to a discriminative verifier trained on the full PRM800K, ThinkPRM scored 8 and 4.5 percentage points higher" and "On a ProcessBench subset, ThinkPRM scored 7.2 percentage points higher than the un-fine-tuned LLM-as-a-Judge", plus "roughly 1% of the 712,000 labels used by the discriminative baseline". These are very precise, paper-level numbers that cannot be checked offline; the writer's report itself lists them under "Needs external verification". Two concerns: (a) the 712,000 figure is not the PRM800K headline count (800K) and its provenance is not explained in the text; (b) the 8 / 4.5 / 7.2 pp gains are stated as bare numbers with only the word "subsets" as a hedge, which is the kind of precision a reader cannot audit. Consider naming the exact table/split and stating that the comparisons hold only on the reported subsets.

2. `docs/chapter17_prm_search/formal-prm.md` — "it also solves 47 of the 658 problems in PutnamBench". The DeepSeek-Prover-V2 paper's PutnamBench split is usually described as having 654 problems (the 540 competition problems plus bonus problems); 658 would need a source. Not verifiable offline — please check the paper's stated total.

3. `docs/chapter17_prm_search/formal-prm.md` (Direction One) — link "Autoformalization with Large Language Models" → `https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12615`. The paper by Yang et al. with that title is arXiv **2205.12628**; if so this link points at an unrelated paper. Please re-check the identifier.

4. `docs/chapter17_prm_search/formal-prm.md` (new "Soundness, Completeness, and Verifier Tests") — using "completeness" for "the checker rejects no valid proof (no false negatives)" is not the standard meaning of completeness in proof theory (which is about a system's proving every true statement) and, more importantly, it is a property of the _search/automation_, not of the kernel: the Lean kernel accepts any proof term handed to it, so it is not the component that has false negatives. The paragraph half-concedes this ("completeness is always bounded in practice by timeouts, tactic inventories, and automation strength"), but as written the reader is told the kernel has a completeness property. Consider renaming to "soundness vs. no-false-negatives (recall of the automation)".

5. `docs/chapter17_prm_search/parallel-reasoning-and-summary.md` (new "From Search Traces to Training Data") — "in _on-policy_ distillation the student generates its own rollouts and the teacher only scores or corrects them". In current usage (on-policy distillation for reasoning models) the teacher produces token-level targets _on the student's own prefixes_, which is a stronger statement than "scores or corrects". The description is not wrong, but it is looser than the term's established meaning; align it with the common usage or say "often called on-policy distillation".

6. `code/chapter17_prm_search/tree_of_thought.py` — the file that the new Exercise 7 tells students to import is labelled "Chapter 13: Tree of Thought (ToT) Reasoning Experiment" in its docstring and in its printed banner, and `code/chapter17_prm_search/requirements.txt` says "# Chapter 13: Future Trends experiment dependencies" while listing `stable-baselines3` and `torch`, which the script never imports (it only needs numpy + matplotlib). The new review page promotes this file as the chapter's lab, so the stale chapter label now surfaces in student-facing material.

7. `docs/chapter17_prm_search/parallel-reasoning-and-summary.md` — PaCoRe is described as a "StepFun, 2026 technical report"; the report's own "Needs external verification" list admits no independent source is available. The internal arithmetic is at least self-consistent (36 paths × 50 K ≈ 1.8 M tokens vs. the reported 1.87 M; 93.7 − 84.1 = 9.6 pp; 1.87 M / 50 K ≈ 37×), so the numbers are coherent, but they should be attributed to the report explicitly at each use rather than in general prose.

## Verdict

The revision is technically careful where it is verifiable: the Pass@8192 fix is correct and the two inverted single-attempt rates now check out exactly ($p = 5.28\%$ and $p = 0.0268\%$, a 197× ratio, as claimed), the standardized $\sqrt{2}$ retelling is mathematically right, and all eight exercises in the new `review.md` reproduce correctly under `.venv/bin/python` (Pass@k inversion, discounted weights, mean/min/product, UCB, majority vote at $N = 5$ and $N = 11$), including the two coding solutions, whose only blemishes are a wrong tie-break description and a last-digit rounding slip. The three new prose inserts (calibration/shortcuts/shift/active collection, soundness/completeness/verifier tests, judge biases and search-trace distillation) are sound but largely untestable assertions, and several paper-level numbers (ThinkPRM +8/+4.5/+7.2 pp, 712 K labels, Prover-V2 47/658, PaCoRe curves) remain stated more precisely than the offline record can support. The most important problems are the missed pre-existing arithmetic error that inflates the PRM800K annotation cost by 3.6× (24,000 h / 12 person-years should be 6,667 h / 3.3 person-years), the wrong majority-vote tally in 17.6 ("−3 appears 4 times" instead of 3), the stale and garbled $\sqrt{2}$ retelling that the standardization pass missed on the very page it edited, and the new review page not being registered in the sidebar (with a `17.R` number that breaks the book's numbering and cannot be synced by `fix:headings`). Those four are cheap to fix and should be fixed before the page ships.
