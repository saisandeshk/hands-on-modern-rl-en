# Review of nt-3

## Errors

1. `docs/chapter16_reasoning/lab.md` — "here $n$ is the pool size, written $N$ in Section 16.3, and $k$ the shortlist size" — The mapping is reversed. In §16.3, $N$ is the number of candidate answers, i.e. the lab's $k$ (the table's `N` column); the lab's $n=64$ is the evaluation pool, a device of the Chen et al. estimator that has no counterpart in §16.3. The note therefore tells the reader that the pool size is the number of candidates and that the candidate count is a "shortlist". Suggested fix: "here $n$ is the pool size (an estimator detail absent from §16.3), $c$ the number correct in the pool, and $k$ the candidate count written $N$ in §16.3".

2. `docs/chapter17_prm_search/discriminative-prm.md` — "$s_i$ denotes the text of step $i$ itself (as distinct from the prefix $o_{\leq i}$ leading up to it)" — This does not resolve the symbol; it creates a double count. The page defines the input as "the reasoning prefix $o_{\leq i}$ up to the current step" and the data format as "response*prefix_up_to_step_i", so $o*{\leq i}$ already contains step $i$. Conditioning on $p_\theta(c\mid q,o_{\leq i},s_i)$ then feeds step $i$ twice. If the prefix is meant to stop before step $i$, the index must be $o_{<i}$ (or $o_{\leq i-1}$), not $o_{\leq i}$. Suggested fix: use $p_\theta(c\mid q,o_{<i},s_i)$ and define $o_{<i}$ as the steps before $i$, or drop $s_i$ and let $o_{\leq i}$ end at step $i$.

3. `docs/chapter17_prm_search/formal-prm.md` — "the 671B model reaches 82.4% ... with Pass@32, and 88.9% when the candidate budget is raised to Pass@8192" — The two numbers cannot both describe the same model under the page's own formula. From 82.4% at $k=32$: $p=1-0.176^{1/32}=5.28\%$. From 88.9% at $k=8192$: $p=1-0.111^{1/8192}=0.0268\%$. But a model with $p=5.28\%$ would get $1-(1-0.0528)^{8192}\approx 1$ at $k=8192$, not 0.889; a model with $p=0.0268\%$ would get $1-(1-0.000268)^{32}\approx 0.0086$ at $k=32$, not 0.824. The implied single-attempt rates differ by about 200×, so the sentence is not a budget-scaling result; the likely cause is that 82.4% belongs to a different model/setting (the project's own fact-check notes that only 88.9% appears in the abstract). Suggested fix: state the model and budget for each figure; if 82.4% is the 7B model, say so; otherwise remove the backward $p$ values and do not conclude "increasing the budget covers more problems" from these two numbers.

4. `docs/chapter13_rlhf/hhh-practice.md` — "after the fourth round, performance typically plateaus" — The page itself says Meta ran three rounds (M1 → M2 → M3) and reports numbers only through M3; the cited paper (Yuan et al., arXiv:2401.10020) has no fourth-round measurement. A fourth-round plateau is therefore stated as an experimental result when it is at best an extrapolation. Suggested fix: "the paper reports gains through M3 and does not test a fourth round; a later plateau is an expectation, not a measured result."

5. `docs/chapter15_grpo/grpo-family.md` — "The original form of GRPO in the R1 paper applied two steps of normalization to the group's reward" — GRPO was introduced in DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024); the R1 paper (2025) uses it. Chapter 15 §15.1 itself says "When the DeepSeekMath paper introduced GRPO", and this page later calls DeepSeekMath Eq. (3) "the original definition". Suggested fix: "The GRPO formulation used in the R1 paper..." or "The original GRPO formulation (DeepSeekMath, 2024) ...".

6. `docs/chapter15_grpo/grpo-practice-and-mechanism.md` — "Normalized advantages: [-1.2, +0.1, +0.8, +0.1, +1.5, +0.1, -0.3, +0.6]" — These cannot be the output of $\hat A_{j,i}=(r_{j,i}-\bar r_j)/(s_j+\epsilon)$: any such vector sums to zero (the deviations from the mean sum to zero), but this list sums to $+1.7$ (mean $+0.2125$). The mid-training list `[0,0,0,-0.5,0,0,0,0]` sums to $-0.5$ and is also impossible for a normalized vector. Suggested fix: recompute the advantages from the rewards actually used for the eight answers, or relabel these lists as illustrative and not normalized.

7. `docs/chapter15_grpo/grpo-practice-and-mechanism.md` — "This book uses the sample standard deviation with Bessel's correction (divide by $G-1$)" — The book-wide claim is contradicted by later chapters. Chapter 16 §16.2 uses `np.std` (default `ddof=0`) and its worked examples use population std: for rewards $(1,1,0,0)$ it reports advantages $(1,1,-1,-1)$, which requires $\sigma=0.5$; the Ch.15 convention ($s=\sqrt{4/3}\approx0.577$) gives $\pm0.866$. Chapter 18's review Exercise 5 explicitly says "standard deviation (population)". The same rewards therefore get different advantage scales in different chapters. Suggested fix: change "This book uses" to "This chapter uses" and add a notation-page note, or unify on one convention.

8. `docs/chapter16_reasoning/lab.md` — "kept 4000 verified self-traces (keep rate 0.25 at T=0.8), mixed them 1:1 with teacher traces" — The code does not mix 1:1. `run_lab.py` sets `n_mix = min(len(kept), len(train_examples))`; with `kept=4000` and `train_examples=2000`, `n_mix=2000`, so the mix is 4000 self + 2000 teacher (2:1), not 1:1. Suggested fix: say "mixed 4000 self-traces with the 2000 teacher traces (2:1; the teacher pool is the limit)", or change the code to sample 4000 teacher traces and update the stated mix.

9. `docs/chapter13_rlhf/ppo-rlhf-loop.md` — "This is the same machinery as in Chapters 6 and 7 on Actor-Critic / PPO" — The chapter numbering is wrong: Chapter 6 is policy gradient, Chapter 7 is actor-critic, and PPO is Chapter 8 (GAE is §8.3). Suggested fix: "Chapters 7 and 8 on actor-critic and PPO", or "Section 8.3 on GAE".

10. `planning/reports/nt-3.md` — "Rendered output identical" (for the $\pi_{\text{ref}}\to\pi_{ref}$ change in `hhh-practice.md`) — This is false. `\pi_{\text{ref}}` renders an upright roman "ref"; `\pi_{ref}` renders three italic math letters. More importantly, the change makes Chapter 13 the only chapter using the non-preferred form while Chapter 14 (after this same revision), Chapter 15, Chapter 18 and Appendix D use `\pi_{\text{ref}}`; the added note documents the deviation instead of removing it. Suggested fix: use `\pi_{\text{ref}}` in Chapter 13 and delete/adjust the note, or keep `\pi_{ref}` but describe it as a deliberate variant and correct the report's "identical" claim.

11. `docs/chapter17_prm_search/outcome-vs-process.md` — "from $p=2k$ it should obtain $4k^2=2q^2$ and hence $2k^2=q^2$, but instead it writes $4k=q^2$, dropping the square on k and the division by 2" — The described slip does not produce the stated faulty line. Dropping the square on $k$ in $4k^2=2q^2$ gives $4k=2q^2$; to get $4k=q^2$ one must additionally halve only the right-hand side. "Skipping the division by 2" would leave $4k=2q^2$, not $4k=q^2$. Suggested fix: "dropping the square on $k$ and halving only the $q^2$ term".

## Doubts

1. `docs/chapter16_reasoning/lab.md` — "Best-of-N with an exact verifier ... follows the coverage curve $1-(1-p)^N$ all the way to 1.00" — The table's exact-verifier values are below the independent-sampling curve for small $N$: at $N=2$ it reports 0.68 while $1-(1-0.52)^2=0.77$; at $N=4$, 0.85 vs 0.95. Samples from the same prompt are correlated, so the i.i.d. coverage formula is an upper bound. "Reproduces the §16.3 picture quantitatively" is therefore too strong; either say "qualitatively", or use the pool's overall rate (pass@1 $=0.47$) and note the within-prompt correlation. (The page's "pool rate $p\approx0.52$" is actually the first-sample rate `p_pool` in the code, while the 64-sample pool rate is 0.47.)

2. `docs/chapter17_prm_search/discriminative-prm.md` — "In the math experiments mentioned above, the minimum step score (min) worked best" — I could not verify this against the cited OpenAI work: _Let's Verify Step by Step_ reranks candidates with the product of the per-step probabilities, not with `min`. If so, `min` is a different (later or other-work) finding and should not be attributed to the MATH experiment discussed here. Please check before keeping the sentence.

3. `docs/chapter18_llm_rl_industrial/modern-industrial-practice.md` — "QK-clip ... clamping it to $[-clip\_value, clip\_value]$ before it enters the Softmax" and "MuonClip ... computes the norm of the update matrix ... scales the matrix proportionally" — In the Kimi K2 report, QK-Clip rescales the query/key projection weights when the maximum attention logit exceeds a threshold; it is not a hard clamp of $QK^\top$ to a fixed interval, and MuonClip is Muon plus QK-Clip, not a separate update-norm clip. A hard clamp would also zero gradients at the bound. Verify the mechanism against §3.1 of the report before keeping both descriptions.

4. `docs/chapter15_grpo/grpo-practice-and-mechanism.md` — "The group size $k$ is the most important hyperparameter in GRPO" — The page defines the group size as $G$ in the formula, code and prose, then switches to $k$ in the "Choosing k" subsection and the comparison table. In Chapters 16–17, $k$ is the pass@$k$ candidate count, a different object. Use $G$ (or $n$, as in Appendix D) throughout.

5. `docs/chapter14_dpo/review.md` — "batch accuracy $a\mapsto 1-a$" under swapping $y_w,y_l$ — This holds only for pairs with $u\neq0$. At $u=0$ both orientations are counted incorrect (`margin > 0` is false either way), so $a=a_{\text{swap}}=0$, not $1-a=1$. The fixture used in Exercise 7 happens to contain no zero margins, so the code test passes, but the stated general identity needs the "for $u\neq0$; ties stay incorrect in both orientations" caveat.

## Verdict

The mechanical notation replacements are mostly safe: the Ch.14/18 symbol changes match the notation page, the added page notes are well placed, and the arithmetic in the touched review exercises (Ch.13, Ch.14, Ch.18) checks out. The revision is let down by the two new explanatory notes: the Ch.16 lab note reverses the $n/N/k$ mapping, and the Ch.17 $s_i$ note defines a term that the page's own $o_{\leq i}$ already contains. The most serious missed error is the Ch.17 Pass@k pair, whose implied single-attempt rates differ by ~200× and cannot be one model's budget scaling. Also worth fixing before merge: the Ch.15 normalized-advantage examples that do not sum to zero, the "book uses sample std" claim contradicted by Ch.16/18, the Ch.16 RFT 1:1 description that the code runs at 2:1, and the unsupported fourth-round Self-Rewarding claim.
