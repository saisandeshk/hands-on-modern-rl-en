## Errors

1. `docs/chapter26_selfplay_frontiers/self-play-outlook/index.md` — "AlphaZero and OpenAI Five both use similar opponent-pool mechanisms." — This is a **changed** sentence (the revision added the words "opponent-pool"), and it is factually wrong about AlphaZero and self-contradictory with the paragraph it sits in. AlphaZero does **not** keep a historical opponent pool: in Silver et al. 2017 the network plays each self-play game "against the most recent version" of itself (league training with historical opponents was introduced later, in AlphaStar and OpenAI Five). The same paragraph's whole point is that "if the latest checkpoint plays only against itself, training can cycle", and the book's own Chapter 7.5 describes exactly that ("Self-play: use the current model plus MCTS to play games"). The revision's own new parenthetical about DeepSeek-R1 ("single-agent training against verifiable rewards rather than against an opponent pool") shows the intended distinction, so citing AlphaZero as an opponent-pool system is doubly wrong: it names the canonical case of *not* using a pool. — Suggested fix: "OpenAI Five (and AlphaStar) use league training, i.e. an opponent pool with regret-based prioritization; AlphaZero itself plays only against its most recent checkpoint, which is why later systems moved to leagues."

2. `docs/chapter26_selfplay_frontiers/offline-rl/index.md` — "naive_q_actor    return=-0.1137 support_gap=0.4819 / cql_actor        return=-0.0420 support_gap=0.0112" — The "Typical output" block does not match the script it is claimed to come from, and the revision's reason for not fixing it is wrong. `minimal_offline_rl_contextual_bandit.py` sets `torch.manual_seed(2)` and `random.seed(2)` at module level, so its output is **deterministic**, not "run-to-run stochasticity (unseeded minibatch sampling)" as the report states. I ran it twice (via `runpy`) and got byte-identical output: `naive_q_actor return=-0.1164 support_gap=0.4857`, `cql_actor return=-0.0429 support_gap=0.0096`, `td3bc_actor return=-0.0260 support_gap=0.0520` (the three lines that *do* match — behaviour policy and oracle — are the deterministic ones). The stale numbers therefore come from an older version of the script, not from noise. A reader who runs the file will see numbers that differ from the page in the last two digits of every line. — Suggested fix: update the block to the values the script actually prints (`-0.1164/0.4857`, `-0.0429/0.0096`, `-0.0260/0.0520`), or state in the report that the snippet is unseeded if determinism is not wanted.

3. `docs/chapter26_selfplay_frontiers/review.md` (Exercise 5, solution) — "Raising $\tau$ toward 1 penalizes positive residuals less and negative residuals more weakly in relative terms, so $V$ moves further into the upper tail…" — The first clause contradicts the formula given two lines above. With $L_2^\tau(u)=|\tau-\mathbf 1(u<0)|u^2$, the weight is **$\tau$ for $u\ge0$** and **$1-\tau$ for $u<0$**. Raising $\tau$ therefore *increases* the penalty on positive residuals (0.8 → 0.9 → 1.0) and *decreases* it on negative residuals (0.2 → 0.1 → 0). The conclusion ("V moves further into the upper tail") is right, but the stated mechanism is inverted: the relative weight positive:negative is $\tau:(1-\tau)$, which grows toward $\infty$ as $\tau\to1$, and that is precisely what pulls $V$ up. (The numbers are fine: $0.2\times4=0.8$, $0.8\times1=0.8$, $0.8\times25=20$, mean $=21.6/3=7.2$.) — Suggested fix: "Raising $\tau$ weights positive residuals more (weight $\tau$) and negative residuals less (weight $1-\tau$), so the expectile moves further into the upper tail of the data-action Q-values."

4. `docs/chapter26_selfplay_frontiers/llm-multi-agent-rl/index.md` (References, twice) — "First end-to-end framework jointly optimizing sampling, training, and orchestration for multi-agent systems." — The revision deliberately softened the body text from "FlexMARL **is the first** end-to-end framework" to "is an end-to-end framework" because the priority claim is unverifiable, but the identical priority claim survives verbatim in **both** reference entries (the plain bullet and the `[^flexmarl]` footnote). The revision is therefore internally inconsistent, and the un-hedged "first" is exactly the kind of claim the report lists as needing external verification. — Suggested fix: delete "First" from both reference entries ("An end-to-end framework that jointly optimizes sampling, training, and orchestration for multi-agent systems"), or state the priority with an explicit source.

5. `docs/chapter26_selfplay_frontiers/self-play-outlook/index.md` (code sketch `self_play_training_loop`) — "opponent = random.choice(model_pool)" — the function now raises `IndexError: Cannot choose from an empty sequence` on the very first iteration with probability 0.2. The pool is only appended to *after* the opponent is chosen (`if i % save_interval == 0: model_pool.append(...)`), so at $i=0$ the 20% branch picks from an empty list (verified: `random.choice([])` → `IndexError`). The revision edited exactly this function (adding `save_interval=10` and the `random`/`numpy` imports) and reported it as fixed, but the snippet is still not runnable as written. — Suggested fix: seed the pool before the loop (e.g. `model_pool.append(current_model.copy())` before the `for`) or guard the branch with `if model_pool and np.random.rand() < 0.8`.

## Doubts

1. `docs/chapter26_selfplay_frontiers/rl-scaling-outlook.md` (26.2.5) — the PRM/ORM table (GSM8K ~82/85/88/90 %, MATH ~40/45/50/55 %) is still a set of round numbers with no source, and the sentence below it now says "several percentage points over ORM on MATH **in the cited setup**" while the paragraph above calls the table "schematic and setup-dependent". "The cited setup" implies the numbers come from Lightman et al. ("Let's Verify Step by Step", the only PRM reference in the file), but they do not correspond to that paper's reported figures as far as I can tell, so the two hedges contradict each other. Either source real numbers (base model, sampling budget) or drop the "in the cited setup" wording.

2. `docs/chapter26_selfplay_frontiers/llm-multi-agent-rl/index.md` — "**ChatDev** is a representative work of this architecture." — the revision deleted "jointly proposed by Tsinghua University and Tsinghua-affiliated startup ModelBest" as unverifiable offline, but ChatDev (arXiv 2307.07924) *is* from Tsinghua University and ModelBest, so the revision removed a correct and checkable attribution. Suggest restoring it with the arXiv reference that is already on the page.

3. `docs/chapter26_selfplay_frontiers/llm-multi-agent-rl/index.md` and `alphaevolve/index.md` — the recent-paper claims (M-GRPO / "Multi-Agent Deep Research" arXiv 2511.13288 with its GAIA / XBench-DeepSearch / WebWalkerQA comparison, SAGE arXiv 2603.15255, MARTI ICLR 2026, FlexMARL arXiv 2602.09578, KD-MARL arXiv 2604.06691, RLMT arXiv 2509.20357 with its "7K prompts / beats 25M-sample Instruct" claim, Genie 3's 720p / 20–24 FPS) cannot be checked offline. ## Verdict

The revision is careful and mostly correct: the cross-chapter attributions (17.1 for ORM/PRM, 13 for the RLHF reference-model KL, 15/16 for RLVR/R1-Zero, Section 7.5 for AlphaGo), the unified hybrid-reward weighting (now consistent with the `0.6/0.4` trainer code), the fixed snippet run path, and the section renumbering are all real improvements, and every numeric example in the new `review.md` that I recomputed (best-of-5 = 0.83193, the M-GRPO advantages 0.508 / −1.397 / 0.889, the expectile loss 7.2, the CQL penalty 1.4644, the majority-vote probability 0.16308, and the two coding exercises) is arithmetically correct. The most serious problems are (1) the newly sharpened "AlphaZero and OpenAI Five both use similar opponent-pool mechanisms", which is wrong about AlphaZero and contradicts the paragraph it sits in; (2) the "Typical output" block of the offline-RL snippet, which does not match the (deterministic) script and was excused with a wrong rationale; (3) the inverted expectile explanation in review.md Exercise 5; (4) the surviving "First end-to-end framework" claim in the FlexMARL reference entries after the body was hedged; (5) the `random.choice` on an empty pool that still crashes the revised self-play sketch; and (6) the new review page not being registered in the sidebar with a non-conforming "26.R" number. Fixing those six plus recording the fixes in `planning/content-review-notes.md` would make this revision sound; the remaining items are paper-verification chores (RLMT/AlphaEvolve/Titans numbers, PRM-vs-ORM table provenance) rather than defects.
