## Errors

1. `docs/chapter26_selfplay_frontiers/lab.md` and `code/chapter26_selfplay_frontiers/tictactoe_self_play.py` — "optimal yardstick and the "draw is the ceiling" certificate." / "a draw against minimax (the ceiling for X) arrives around episode 6000" / "X beats random play quickly and holds" / "so "draw vs minimax" is the ceiling for X" — a draw against the *game-theoretic* minimax player is **not** a certificate that X's policy is optimal (or unexploitable). Writing $v(\pi,\sigma)$ for X's payoff, the minimax player $\sigma^*$ satisfies $v(\pi,\sigma^*)\le 0$ for every $\pi$, but optimality requires $\min_\sigma v(\pi,\sigma)=0$ against the best response *to $\pi$*, and $\sigma_{\mathrm{BR}}(\pi)\ne\sigma^*$ in general. I computed that best response exactly (a small search in which X plays its greedy policy and O minimizes X's payoff; validated on two controls: a perfect X that plays `minimax_move` gets 0.0, a "first empty cell" X gets -1.0). For the documented default curriculum (`--opp-random 0.3`, seed 0) X's greedy policy is **beaten at every logged checkpoint** (1500, 3000, …, 30000): $v(\pi,\sigma_{\mathrm{BR}})=-1.0$ throughout, including at episode 30000 where the minimax game is a draw. A concrete winning line for O at episode 30000 is X0, O5, X1, O2, X8, O4, X6, O3 (O takes the row 3-4-5), i.e. the "draw" checkpoint is a policy that a tailored opponent beats outright. The same computation on the pure-random curriculum (`--opp-random 1.0`) gives $v(\pi,\sigma_{\mathrm{BR}})=0.0$ at episode 30000, so the random-only curriculum actually reaches the ceiling while the default mixed curriculum never does — the opposite of what the page's narrative implies, and the opposite of Exercise 4's hint ("both curricula reach a draw"). Suggested fix: evaluate X against the best response to X's own policy (or report both numbers: "the minimax player can only draw X, but X is still exploitable by a tailored opponent"), drop the word "certificate", and rewrite the "Weak opponents teach fast, strong opponents certify" bullet and Exercise 4's hint accordingly.

5. `code/chapter26_selfplay_frontiers/rps_self_play.py` and `docs/chapter26_selfplay_frontiers/lab.md` — "Simultaneous projected gradient ascent on each player's payoff." / "while projected gradient ascent steps both strategies simultaneously:" — the code does **not** update simultaneously. In `run_gradient` the first line reassigns `pi` and the second line then uses that *already updated* `pi`:
   ```python
   pi = project_simplex(pi + lr * (utility @ sigma))
   sigma = project_simplex(sigma - lr * (utility.T @ pi))   # uses the NEW pi
   ```
   That is a sequential (Gauss–Seidel) update; the simultaneous (Jacobi) version would need the previous `pi`. The qualitative conclusion survives (I re-ran both variants: the sequential one cycles with a current Nash gap ≈ 0.89, the simultaneous one with ≈ 1.00, and both time averages converge to (1/3, 1/3, 1/3)), and the reported 0.8947 is the sequential value, so only the description is wrong. Suggested fix: either store `pi_old` and update `sigma` from it (then re-run the reference number, which becomes ≈ 1.00), or relabel the dynamics "alternating/sequential projected gradient" in the docstring and on the page.

6. `docs/chapter26_selfplay_frontiers/lab.md` (Exercise 1) — "Hint: redo the two indifference equations from the" / "equilibria section; only the $p_R$ equation changes." — the hint is wrong: with a Rock-win payoff of $k$ the indifference chain is $-p_P + k\,p_S = p_R - p_S = -k\,p_R + p_P$, so the coefficient $k$ appears in **two** of the three expressions (multiplying $p_S$ and $p_R$). Changing only one of them gives the wrong answer: for $k=3$ one gets $(p_R,p_P,p_S) = (0.2, 0.55, 0.25)$ instead of the true $(0.2, 0.6, 0.2)$. The correct general solution is $p_R = p_S = 1/(k+2)$, $p_P = k/(k+2)$ with game value 0 (I verified numerically that these profiles have Nash gap 0 for $k=1.5$, $2$ and $3$), i.e. $(2/7, 3/7, 2/7)$ for $k=1.5$ and $(1/5, 3/5, 1/5)$ for $k=3$. Suggested fix: replace the hint with "both indifference equations change: the Rock-win payoff $k$ appears once as $k p_S$ and once as $-k p_R$; solve $-p_P + k p_S = p_R - p_S = -k p_R + p_P$ with $p_R+p_P+p_S=1$ to get $p_R=p_S=1/(k+2)$, $p_P=k/(k+2)$".

## Doubts

1. `docs/chapter26_selfplay_frontiers/lab.md` (and `code/chapter26_selfplay_frontiers/kuhn_cfr.py`) — "exploitability" is used with two different definitions in the same lab: in part 1 it is explicitly the Nash gap, `max_a (U sigma)_a - min_b (pi^T U)_b` (so the RPS table's "Current exploitability 2.0000" is a NashConv), while in part 2 the page says to "halve their difference" (the code docstring: "Exploitability (NashConv / 2) is 0 exactly at equilibrium."), so Kuhn's "0.0016" is half a NashConv. Each part defines its own convention locally, but the two tables sit side by side and the reader is invited to compare 2.00 with 0.0016. A one-line note ("part 1 reports the Nash gap, part 2 reports exploitability = NashConv/2") would remove the ambiguity.

2. `docs/chapter26_selfplay_frontiers/lab.md` — "**Regret follows the money.** In Kuhn poker the Q-call coordinate moves slowly because it sits on a near-indifference ridge: deviating gains almost nothing per visit, so regret accumulates slowly there…" — at the equilibrium this is *exact* indifference, not "near": with the posterior 1/4 Jack, 3/4 King, $EV(\text{call}) = 2(1/4 - 3/4) = -1 = EV(\text{fold})$, so deviating gains exactly nothing in expectation. The slow drift of the average's Q-call coordinate (0.5653 at 2000 iterations, 0.5613 at 20 000, 0.5553 at 100 000, against the CFR limit ≈ 0.5545) is better explained by the equilibrium set being a *continuum* (a ridge of equilibria) that the average strategy approaches slowly, not by "almost nothing per visit".

3. `docs/chapter26_selfplay_frontiers/lab.md` — "Full lab (about 20 seconds on a laptop CPU at seed 0)" vs `code/chapter26_selfplay_frontiers/README.md` — "Full lab (everything, a few minutes on a laptop CPU)" and the per-script row "~5 s". On this machine the four documented commands take 11 s (RPS standard), 11 s (RPS biased), 5 s (Kuhn) and 9 s (tic-tac-toe), i.e. ~35 s in total, so the two new files disagree about the same run and the README's per-script "~5 s" is optimistic by roughly a factor of two.

4. `docs/chapter26_selfplay_frontiers/lab.md` — the tic-tac-toe evaluation plays a **single** game against the deterministic minimax opponent ("`evaluate(learner, "minimax", 1, args.seed)`"), and the table's "Vs minimax" column is that one game. Because the greedy policy and the minimax tie-breaks are deterministic, the number is reproducible, but one game is a thin basis for the page's claims about "the optimal player"; even the "O wins" rows only certify that *this* optimal opponent found a loss (a necessary, not sufficient, condition for optimality), which is the same gap as finding 1.

5. `planning/reports/ch26-lab.md` — "Needs external verification: None. All game-theoretic claims were re-derived by hand and confirmed numerically." — true for the Kuhn equilibrium family and the RPS equilibria (I re-derived all five indifference conditions and confirmed the family has zero exploitability and value $-1/18$ for every $\alpha$, and confirmed the biased-RPS equilibrium $(1/4,1/2,1/4)$), but the tic-tac-toe conclusion ("draws minimax from ~18000 episodes") was verified only against the game-theoretic minimax player, which is exactly the measurement that turns out not to certify optimality (finding 1).

## Verdict

The game theory in this lab is unusually solid: I re-derived the five Kuhn indifference conditions from scratch (including the sign bookkeeping on the "cb" posterior), confirmed that the shipped `equilibrium_profile(alpha)` has exactly zero exploitability and value $-1/18$ for every $\alpha\in[0,1/3]$, confirmed the biased-RPS equilibrium $(1/4,1/2,1/4)$ and its generalisation $p_R=p_S=1/(k+2)$, and every published number in the two tables reproduces exactly from the shipped code (RPS: 2.0000/0.0001/0.0001, 0.8947/0.0001, 2.0000/0.0130/≈0.008, biased 0.0003 and 0.0146, BR average exploitable by 0.6667; Kuhn: 0.0119 at iteration 200, 0.0016 and NashConv 0.0032 at 20 000, value $-0.055555$, J 0.2211 / K 0.6605 / ratio 2.987 / Q-call 0.5613 / P2 bluff 0.3325 / P2 Q-call 0.3411, the wiggle 0.0015 at 10 000 and 0.0024 at 10 200; tic-tac-toe: the four table rows and the O-wins/draw pattern). The 10 unit tests pass, the CFR log-log slope is -0.51, so Exercise 3's $1/\sqrt{T}$ hint is right, and Exercise 4's premise holds (the pure-random curriculum reaches a minimax draw at episode 1500). The important problem is the tic-tac-toe conclusion: the page sells "draw vs minimax" as a "certificate" that X has reached the ceiling, but X's greedy policy is still beaten by the best response to itself at every checkpoint (-1.0 for X), including the 30 000-episode "draw" one, where a five-move O line wins; the random-only curriculum is the one that actually becomes unexploitable. Second, the page repeats the `N.L` numbering mistake already flagged for `7.R` and `10.L` (it should be 26.6) and is still missing from the sidebar, and it points at "the regret-minimization methods surveyed in Section 26.1", a section that never mentions CFR or regret minimization. Third, two smaller technical slips: the "simultaneous" projected-gradient update is actually sequential, and Exercise 1's hint ("only the $p_R$ equation changes") sends the student to the wrong equilibrium for a general Rock-win payoff. Fix the tic-tac-toe claim (and the exercise hint), renumber the page as 26.6 and register it, correct the Section 26.1 reference, and correct the gradient wording and Exercise 1's hint.
