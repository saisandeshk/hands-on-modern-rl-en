# Review of ch11-lab

## Errors

1. `code/chapter11_imitation_meta_rl/bc_dagger_cartpole.py` (module docstring) — "It reaches expert return with far fewer expert labels than BC, i.e. the O(T eps) behaviour." — False for this experiment, and a mis-statement of the theory. I re-ran the script (`--seed 0`, CPU) and got the lab's numbers exactly: BC reaches the expert return 500.0 with **1000** expert labels (N = 2 trajectories x 500 steps; all three seeds individually score 500.0), while DAgger first reaches a 500.0 mean return at iteration 2 with a mean of **2365** expert labels (seed 1 alone: 500 + 597 + 1000 = 2097). DAgger therefore spends ~2.4x more expert labels than BC to reach the same return. The script's own third figure panel ("Label efficiency: BC vs DAgger", `save_plots`) plots BC at (1000 labels, 500 return) and DAgger at (2365 labels, 500 return), i.e. the figure shows the opposite of the sentence. On top of that, the O(T eps) bound of Ross et al. (2011) bounds the _number of mistakes_ of the learned policy, not the number of expert labels; DAgger spends one expert label per environment step, so it cannot be label-cheaper than BC in this setting. Suggested fix: replace the sentence with "reaches expert return from one _complete_ demonstration episode plus on-policy corrections (a BC policy trained on the same single episode scores 377.7), i.e. DAgger repairs a policy that BC cannot fix from the same data", and retitle the third panel ("expert-label budget" instead of "label efficiency") or drop it.

2. `docs/chapter11_imitation_meta_rl/lab.md` — "reaching expert return from a single demonstration trajectory" (What you will learn) and "using one full expert trajectory where reliable BC needs two" (What to look for) — the label accounting is misleading as stated. By the page's own DAgger table, 500.0 is first reached at iteration 2, after 500 + 865 + 1000 = 2365 expert labels on average (seed 1: 500 + 597 + 1000 = 2097), i.e. 4.2-4.7 expert-trajectory-equivalents, against BC's 2 trajectories = 1000 labels. What DAgger saves is the number of _complete expert demonstration episodes_ (one continuous episode instead of two), not the number of labels. Suggested fix: give both numbers explicitly ("one demonstration episode + 1865 correction labels, versus BC's two episodes = 1000 labels") or weaken the wording to "from a single demonstration episode plus on-policy corrections".

3. `docs/chapter11_imitation_meta_rl/lab.md` — "The second agreement row confirms the mechanism directly: agreement measured on the learner's _own_ states is consistently below agreement on expert states" — the per-seed data of the reference run contradict "consistently", and they contradict it for exactly the seed the page highlights. From my re-run: seed 0 -> own 0.736 < exp 0.844; seed 1 -> own 0.800 **> exp 0.775**; seed 2 -> own 0.761 < exp 0.851. Only the 3-seed mean (0.766 < 0.823) supports the statement. The failing seed is the interesting case and the page gets its direction wrong there: a policy that has fallen into a stable-but-wrong behaviour agrees with the expert _more_ on the states it visits than the expert does on the expert's own trajectory. Suggested fix: replace "consistently below" with "on average below (2 of the 3 seeds; for the failing seed 1 it is slightly higher)" and show the per-seed numbers, or drop the claim for seed 1.

4. `docs/chapter11_imitation_meta_rl/lab.md` — "the discriminator numbers reproduce the table in Section 11.2.3.2 numerically" — they do not. The chapter's table is computed from the _optimal_ discriminator $D^*(s,a) = p_{expert}/(p_{expert}+p_\pi)$ with counts 800/200 ($D^* = 0.8$, $-\log 0.2 \approx 1.61$) and 0/500 ($D^* = 0$, reward 0). The lab measures 3.451 (expert pairs) and 0.502 (learner pairs) from a _learned_ discriminator whose held-out accuracy is only 0.739/0.819, i.e. far from $D^*$, so the two sets of numbers are not comparable; and 0.502 is not "near zero" next to the table's 0. Suggested fix: say the run "illustrates the pattern of the table in Section 11.2.3.2 (several units of reward on expert pairs, ~0 on learner-only pairs) but not its values: the table uses the optimal $D^*$, while the trained discriminator here is far from optimal (accuracy 0.74/0.82)".

5. `docs/chapter11_imitation_meta_rl/lab.md` — "**DAgger is the aggregate loop of Section 11.1.3, verbatim:**" — the script is not the chapter's loop. (i) The chapter's pseudocode mixes expert actions into the rollouts with a decaying $\beta$ (`beta = max(0.0, 1.0 - it / 10)`); `run_dagger` has no $\beta$ at all (the page's own exercise 3 has to add it). (ii) The chapter keeps training the _same_ network on the enlarged dataset (`train_bc(policy_net, dataset)`), while the script discards the policy every iteration (`policy = PolicyNet()`), i.e. it retrains from scratch. (iii) The chapter collects 50 trajectories per iteration, the script 2. Suggested fix: "the aggregate loop of Section 11.1.3, with three simplifications: no $\beta$-mixing, a fresh network each iteration, and 2 rollouts per iteration".

6. `docs/chapter11_imitation_meta_rl/lab.md` (exercise 6) — "Hint: use $1 - $ agreement on own states as $\epsilon$ and plot $500 - $ return versus $\epsilon$" — the two inline math spans do not render as math on this site, so the hint will show stray dollar signs. `docs/.vitepress/config.mjs` defines `isValidMathDelimiter` with `canClose: prevChar !== 0x20 && prevChar !== 0x09 && (prevChar < 0x30 || prevChar > 0x39)`, i.e. the character before the closing `$` must not be a space; in `$1 - $` and `$500 - $` it is a space, so the `math_inline` rule bails out and the `$` is emitted as literal text. I simulated the rule over the exact sentence: the two `$\epsilon$` spans parse, `$1 - $` and `$500 - $` do not. The book escapes literal dollars as `\$` elsewhere (e.g. `docs/chapter13_rlhf/hhh-practice.md`). Suggested fix: write `$1-\text{agreement}$` / `$500-\text{return}$`, or drop the math ("1 minus agreement", "500 minus return").

7. `docs/.vitepress/config.mjs` / `docs/chapter11_imitation_meta_rl/lab.md` — the new page is not registered in the sidebar, and its number does not follow the book's scheme. The Chapter 11 block of `sidebar` still lists only 11.1/11.2/11.3, so `/chapter11_imitation_meta_rl/lab` is unreachable from the site navigation (and from the PDF/EPUB builders that walk the sidebar); AGENTS.md requires "New pages go under the right `docs/chapterNN_*` directory and must be added to the sidebar in `docs/.vitepress/config.mjs`". The report defers this to a coordinator, but as committed the page is orphaned. The proposed number "11.L" is also not an `X.Y` number: `scripts/fix-heading-numbers.mjs` matches sidebar entries with `/text:\s*'((?:[A-Z]\.)?\d+(?:\.\d+)*)\s+([^']+)'/`, which cannot match "11.L", so the page's H1 (`# 11.L ...`) and front-matter title can never be synced with the sidebar (every other page in the book uses a purely numeric number: 11.1, 11.2, ..., D.1.1). Suggested fix: add `{ text: '11.4 Lab: Behaviour Cloning versus DAgger', link: '/chapter11_imitation_meta_rl/lab' }` to the Chapter 11 sidebar block, rename the front-matter title and the H1 to "11.4 ...", and add a back-link from 11.1/11.2.

## Doubts

1. `planning/reports/ch11-lab.md` — "Diagnosed by sweeping step budgets (300 -> 2000 steps: expert/learner separation 0.83/0.95) and fixed by raising the full config to 2000 updates; final full run gives 0.739/0.819 and 3.451 vs 0.502." As written, the same configuration (2000 discriminator updates) is reported with two different outcomes, and the final one is _worse_ than the sweep's, so the "fix" narrative does not hold. Presumably the sweep used a different seed or a different data split, but the report should say so. (The final numbers themselves reproduce exactly: I re-ran the script with `--seed 0` and got accuracy 0.739/0.819 and rewards 3.451/0.502.)

2. `docs/chapter11_imitation_meta_rl/lab.md` (exercise 6) — "First-order check of the $T^2$ claim ... test whether the shortfall grows super-linearly in the error" — the $T^2$ factor cannot be probed this way: $T$ is fixed at 500, so $O(T^2\epsilon)$ is _linear_ in $\epsilon$ (and numerically vacuous here: $500^2 \times 0.18 \approx 4.5\times10^4$, i.e. more mistakes than an episode has steps). What the suggested plot can show is that the shortfall grows faster than linearly in $\epsilon$, which is a different (and correct) statement about compounding. With the lab's own five BC points the plot is degenerate anyway: $(1-\text{agree}_{\text{own}},\ 500-\text{return}) = (0.234, 122.3), (0.220, 0), (0.034, 0), (0.011, 0), (0.006, 0)$ - four of the five points sit at zero. Suggested reframing: "check the compounding effect: does the return shortfall grow faster than linearly in the per-step error?" and vary the horizon separately to see the $T^2$ scaling.

3. `docs/chapter11_imitation_meta_rl/lab.md` (exercise 4) — "Hint: normalise the per-episode discounted rewards as advantages" is imprecise: REINFORCE needs a per-timestep advantage (return-to-go minus a baseline), not one scalar per episode, and normalising returns is variance reduction, not an advantage. The hint would also benefit from saying _why_ the from-scratch REINFORCE learner stalls: the discriminator in `gail_discriminator_cartpole.py` was trained to separate the expert from a _weak BC policy_, so a from-scratch policy visits states the discriminator has never seen and gets ~0 reward everywhere. I reproduced the stall with a frozen discriminator (40 REINFORCE iterations, 5 episodes each, normalised returns): mean return of the last 10 episodes 22.7, best episode 53 - so the page's "stalls below return 100" claim itself holds.

4. `docs/chapter11_imitation_meta_rl/lab.md` and `code/chapter11_imitation_meta_rl/README.md` — both use the British "behaviour cloning" while the chapter and the rest of the book use "behavior cloning" ("11.1 Behavior Cloning and Interactive Imitation Learning"; `docs/chapter11_imitation_meta_rl/bc-dagger.md` uses "behavior" throughout). Terminology consistency.

5. `docs/chapter11_imitation_meta_rl/lab.md` — the cross-references "11.1.1", "11.1.2", "11.1.3", "11.2.3.1", "11.2.3.2", "11.2.4.1" merge the page number with the in-page section number. In this book "Section 11.2" denotes the _page_ 11.2, so "11.2.3" can be misread as a page id; the only precedent is "Section 6.3.2" in `docs/chapter06_policy_gradient/dice-game.md`. Consider "Section 2 of 11.1" / "Section 3.2 of 11.2".

6. `docs/chapter11_imitation_meta_rl/lab.md` — the "agreement on expert states" metric is measured on 5 fresh expert trajectories (`collect_expert_trajectories(5, start_seed=50_000 + seed)`), but since the expert is a deterministic controller and CartPole's initial state is uniform in $\pm0.05$, those "held-out" states are essentially the training distribution, so 0.823 is a training-set-like number rather than a generalization number. The code comment ("Held-out expert states for the 'agreement on expert states' metric") oversells it.

7. Runtime is stated three ways: "~10 min" (script docstring), "~8 min" (README and the page), "472 s" (report); the full main script took 72 s on the review machine. The GAIL script is quoted as 5.6 s / "6 s" / "<10 s" while I measured 19.7 s. Machine-dependent, but the statements should agree with each other.

8. `code/chapter11_imitation_meta_rl/requirements.txt` — the file omits `pytest`, although both the page and the README instruct the reader to run `.venv/bin/pytest code/chapter11_imitation_meta_rl -q`; `code/chapter10_offline_rl/requirements.txt` lists `pytest>=7.0.0` for exactly this reason.

9. `docs/chapter11_imitation_meta_rl/lab.md` — the compounding-error story is not cleanly separated from underfitting. The N=1 policy has $\epsilon \approx 0.18$ _on expert states_ (it is badly underfit, not merely shifted), so the jump from 377.7 (N=1) to 500.0 (N=2) is equally explained by a smaller approximation error. The direct evidence for compounding is only the expert-state vs own-state agreement gap (0.823 -> 0.766), which is modest and not consistent across seeds (see error 3). A cleaner demonstration would show that the per-step error rate itself grows with the step index (which is what exercise 1 proposes).

10. `planning/reports/ch11-lab.md` — "Needs external verification: None. All paper/year claims cited in the lab page point at the chapter's own Further Reading list (Ross et al. 2011, Ho & Ermon 2016)". The lab page in fact cites no papers at all (it only cross-references sections), so the sentence describes something that is not there. Harmless, but the report should describe the material that exists.

## Verdict

The new material is careful and, in the main, reproducible: I re-ran both scripts on the review machine and every number quoted on the page and in the README reproduces exactly (BC 377.7/0.823/0.766 for N=1; DAgger labels 500/1365/2365/3365/4365 and returns 377.7/483.8/500/500/500; seed 1 going 133.1 -> 451.4 -> 500.0; discriminator accuracy 0.739/0.819 and implicit rewards 3.451/0.502), the hand-written PD expert really does score 500/500 (I checked 300 seeds), the 8 pytest tests pass, and the "REINFORCE on the frozen discriminator reward stalls below 100" claim reproduces (I got a mean return of ~23 after 40 iterations). The serious problems are (1) the central claim of the lab - that DAgger reaches expert return with fewer expert labels than BC - is contradicted by the lab's own numbers (1000 labels for BC vs 2365 for DAgger) and by the script's own third figure panel, and the O(T eps) bound is mis-attributed to a label count; (2) the page's headline mechanism claim ("own-state agreement is consistently below expert-state agreement") is falsified by the per-seed data for the very seed the page discusses; (3) the claim that the discriminator numbers "reproduce the table in Section 11.2.3.2 numerically" compares a learned, far-from-optimal discriminator against the table's optimal D\*; (4) "the aggregate loop of Section 11.1.3, verbatim" is not verbatim (no $\beta$-mixing, from-scratch retraining, 2 rollouts per iteration); (5) the new page is not in the sidebar and uses an "11.L" number that `scripts/fix-heading-numbers.mjs` cannot parse, so it is unreachable and can never be auto-synced; and (6) two inline-math spans in exercise 6 (`$1 - $`, `$500 - $`) do not render as math under this site's delimiter rule. The remaining items (the report's inconsistent discriminator-sweep numbers, the imprecise REINFORCE hint, the British "behaviour" spelling, the ambiguous "11.1.2"-style references, the missing `pytest` requirement, and the underfitting-vs-compounding attribution) are smaller and mostly cosmetic, but should be cleaned up before the page is merged.
