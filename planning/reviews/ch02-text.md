# Review of ch02-text

Scope: `git diff english-only...w/ch02-text` (bandit.md, mdp.md, policy-value.md, new review.md) plus the writer's own report. All numeric claims below were recomputed with `.venv/bin/python -c`; the bandit simulations re-implement `code/chapter02_rl_basics/two_armed_bandit.py` verbatim (200 runs x 1000 steps, `np.random.seed(run)` per run).

## Errors

1. `docs/chapter02_rl_basics/review.md` (Exercise 9 solution) — "UCB ($c = 2$) is competitive with Thompson sampling on this easy two-arm gap; the clear gap is between both directed methods and undirected epsilon exploration" — this is contradicted by the chapter's own code. Running the chapter's `strategy_ucb(bandit, n_steps, c=2.0)` exactly as written (bonus `c*sqrt(log t/N_a)`, first two pulls forced) for 200 x 1000 steps gives mean total **564.2** (regret **35.8**), versus epsilon-greedy 587.9 (regret 12.1) and Thompson 592.0 (regret 8.0). The c=2 rule pulls the worse arm ~185 times out of 1000, because the chapter's bonus `sqrt(ln t / N_a)` lacks the `sqrt(2)` of standard UCB1, so `c=2` over-explores (with `c=1`: total 584.2; with the `sqrt(2)` form: 574.3; with `c=0.5`: 594.7). So for the configuration the exercise tells the reader to run, UCB is _worse_ than plain epsilon-greedy, not "competitive" with Thompson, and the concluding generalization ("directed exploration (UCB, Thompson) should beat fixed-epsilon randomness") does not hold for the prescribed setting. — Suggested fix: report the measured numbers (UCB c=2 ~564, eps-greedy ~588, Thompson ~592), or change the exercise to `c=1`/`c=0.5` (or to the `sqrt(2 ln t/N_a)` form) before claiming UCB beats fixed-epsilon exploration.

2. `docs/chapter02_rl_basics/review.md` (Exercise 9 reference result) — "Thompson sampling totals about 594-595 (regret $\approx$ 5-6)" — not reproducible. With 200 seeds x 1000 steps I measure Thompson totals of **590.0-593.6** (mean ~592, regret ~8) across several seeding schemes (per-run seed: 592.0; shared RNG stream: 593.0; per-strategy seed: 592.6); 12 unseeded repetitions of the script-style experiment gave 590.0-593.6. The per-run standard deviation is 15.6, so the standard error of the 200-run mean is ~1.1: the quoted "594-595" band is a 1-point window at the optimistic edge of the noise, i.e. stated more precisely than the experiment supports. — Suggested fix: quote "about 592 (regret ~8)" or "roughly 590-594 (regret 6-10)", with the seed-dependence caveat that is already there.

3. `docs/chapter02_rl_basics/mdp.md` — "CartPole runs for up to 200 steps" versus the newly added "CartPole's 500-step cap fired while the pole was still balanced" on the same page — direct self-contradiction introduced by the new truncation subsection. Gymnasium `CartPole-v1` has a 500-step limit, and Chapter 1 (`docs/chapter01_cartpole/training.md`) documents exactly that ("`truncated=True` means the 500-step time limit was reached", final score `500.0 ± 0.0`); `policy-value.md` and `review.md` also use T=500. — Suggested fix: change "CartPole runs for up to 200 steps" to "up to 500 steps".

4. `docs/chapter02_rl_basics/mdp.md` (new reward-shaping paragraph) — "a bonus proportional to how far right the cart sits, intended to encourage centering correction ... An agent maximizing that shaped reward may park the cart at the right edge and let the pole wobble, collecting the position bonus while surviving fewer steps" — two defects. (a) The bonus as written is a rightward bias, not a centering signal: a term proportional to $-|x|$ would be what "encourages centering correction". (b) The described behaviour does not maximize the shaped reward: with $r_t = 1 + c\,x_t$, the shaped-return maximizer keeps the cart at the right edge _and survives as long as possible_, because every extra step adds at least the +1 survival term; there is no reason to end the episode early, so "surviving fewer steps" is wrong. The conclusion ("the shaped reward's optimal policy is no longer the true task's optimal policy") does hold, but not for the reason given. — Suggested fix: use a shaping term whose optimum really differs (e.g. a bonus for touching the right edge, or $-c|x|$ for centering) and describe the resulting behaviour correctly (e.g. "keeps the cart parked at the right edge instead of near the centre").

5. `docs/chapter02_rl_basics/mdp.md` (new "Termination Versus Truncation" subsection) — "the return $G_0$ counts only the 500 observed rewards, but a learner estimating $V(s_{500})$ must treat it as '500 rewards so far plus whatever balancing longer would add'" — conflates two different quantities. $V(s_{500})$ is the discounted value _of the state at the cutoff_ (what remains to be earned from there), not "the rewards so far plus more"; the object that mixes past and future is the bootstrapped return $G_0 \approx \sum_{k<500}\gamma^k r_k + \gamma^{500}V(s_{500})$. — Suggested fix: say that the truncated episode's target must bootstrap with $\gamma^{500}V(s_{500})$ instead of 0, while $V(s_{500})$ itself is simply the continuation value.

6. `docs/chapter02_rl_basics/review.md` (new page) — the page is not registered in `docs/.vitepress/config.mjs`, so it is unreachable from the site navigation; the branch's report defers this ("coordinator to insert and renumber"). AGENTS.md requires every navigable page to be listed in the hand-written sidebar. The chosen number "2.R" also breaks the `N.k` convention used by every other page and is invisible to `scripts/fix-heading-numbers.mjs`, whose sidebar regex only matches `\d+\.\d+`. — Suggested fix: add a sidebar entry under Chapter 2 (e.g. "2.4 Summary and Exercises" -> `/chapter02_rl_basics/review`) and align the front-matter title and H1 with it.

## Doubts

1. `docs/chapter02_rl_basics/mdp.md` (new truncation subsection) — "Truncation means an external limit cut the interaction short: ... or a training loop collected a fixed-length rollout slice." A rollout boundary is not an episode truncation: in the Chapter 1 PPO script the environment is _not_ reset at the rollout boundary, so the episode continues across iterations, and Gymnasium emits no `truncated` flag there. Calling it truncation is defensible only in the loose "cut experience into training batches" sense used later in the same paragraph. — Consider restricting the definition to genuine episode cutoffs and mentioning rollout boundaries separately.

2. `docs/chapter02_rl_basics/mdp.md` (pre-existing, in the writer's page) — the transition example "$P(\theta' = 0.03 \mid \theta = 0.05, \text{push right}) \approx 0.7$" / "$\approx 0.3$" contradicts the same page's statement that "in CartPole, the dynamics $P$ are determined by Newtonian mechanics" (CartPole is deterministic, so such a conditional probability is 0 or 1). — Consider labelling the example as an illustrative stochastic environment instead of CartPole.

3. `docs/chapter02_rl_basics/review.md` (Exercise 8 solution) — "its advantage (vanishing late exploration) only dominates over longer horizons. ... Trying a faster decay (e.g. 0.99) or a longer horizon flips the comparison." I verified this qualitatively (decay 0.995 at 10 000 steps: regret 27 vs fixed-eps 94; decay 0.99 at 1000 steps: regret 12 vs 14), but the exercise text does not fix the horizon/seeds, so "the ordering ... is robust for this horizon and decay rate" is a claim about an experiment the reader must set up; the given reference numbers (fixed 588 / decay 579) reproduce exactly under `np.random.seed(run)` per run (587.9 / 578.8), so I consider this acceptable, but the "robust" wording depends on an unspecified protocol.

4. `docs/chapter02_rl_basics/policy-value.md` (new bridge note) — the note correctly states that the page uses $R_{t+1}$ (Sutton & Barto) while Section 2.2 uses $r_t$, but the same page also uses $R(s,a)$ and $R(s,a,s')$ for the _reward function_, so $R$ is overloaded for two different objects within one page (reward variable vs. reward function). Not wrong, but easy to misread.

## Verdict

The revision is careful and mostly correct: the discount-table fix (0.39 -> 0.039) is right, the Lai & Robbins attribution is right, the notation harmonisation ($(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},\ldots)$, $\mathbb{R}^4$) and the reward-index bridge note are appropriate, and the vast majority of the numeric examples in the three pages and in `review.md` (binomial 11.1%, misjudgment 12.8%, 52/48 -> 34%/17%, UCB 0.95/1.51, decay schedule 0.61/0.082/0.0067, CartPole returns 3.439/2.71/1.75/99.34/63.40/86.60, effective-horizon fractions 65.13/63.40/63.23% and $1-e^{-1}$, explore-then-commit 16/-16/11.9, V-Q and advantage arithmetic) reproduce exactly when recomputed. The most serious problem is the new coding exercise: its Exercise 9 solution asserts that the chapter's UCB with $c=2$ is "competitive with Thompson sampling" and beats fixed-epsilon exploration, but running the chapter's own code gives UCB $c=2 \approx 564$ total versus epsilon-greedy $\approx 588$ and Thompson $\approx 592$ (regret 36 vs 12 vs 8), and its "594-595 / regret 5-6" Thompson figure does not reproduce (measured 590-594, regret ~8). Secondary but real issues are the newly created internal contradiction on the CartPole episode length (200 vs 500 steps), an incorrect behavioural claim in the new reward-shaping paragraph, a conflation of $V(s_{500})$ with a bootstrapped truncated return, and a new page that is not in the sidebar and therefore unreachable. Fix the Exercise 8/9 reference numbers and claims, the 200/500-step inconsistency, the shaping example, and register the new page, and this branch would be in good shape.
