## Doubts

1. `intrinsic-motivation-exploration.md` — "The per-step success probability is then at most $0.1 \times (1/6) = 1/60$" — the bound points the wrong way. Under $\epsilon$-greedy, $P(\text{take the required action}) = (1-\epsilon)\cdot\mathbf 1[\text{greedy}=a^*] + \epsilon/6$, so it is _at least_ $\epsilon/6 = 1/60$ and at most $(1-\epsilon)+\epsilon/6 = 0.917$; $(1/60)^{50}=1.24\times10^{-89}$ is the probability of executing the 50-step sequence _by random action only_ (exactly right only if the greedy action never coincides with the required one), not an upper bound on the success probability. The intended reading ("only the random branch can discover an unknown sequence") is sound, but "at most" should become "if every one of the 50 steps has to come from the random branch" / "at least".

2. `intrinsic-motivation-exploration.md` — "in the tabular setting this bonus provably drives the agent to cover the state space (it is the reward-bonus counterpart of UCB-style algorithms such as MBIE)" — the coverage guarantee for bonus-based exploration is a statement about a policy that acts (near-)greedily with respect to _optimistic_ value estimates $\hat Q(s,a)+\beta/\sqrt{N}$; a learner that merely adds $\beta/\sqrt{N}$ to the reward and behaves greedily w.r.t. its own (non-optimistic) estimates has no such guarantee. The parenthetical also introduces "MBIE" without expansion and without a reference (the chapter's Further Reading list has no entry for it), and the count is over states $N(s_t)$ whereas the count-based bonus of Bellemare et al. 2016 counts state–action pairs $N(s,a)$. Suggest: "acting greedily with respect to optimistic value estimates, this bonus provably drives the agent to cover the state space in the tabular case (the reward-bonus counterpart of UCB-style model-based exploration such as MBIE)", plus a reference for MBIE.

3. `intrinsic-motivation-exploration.md` — "This is why novelty-seeking objectives explicitly reward visiting states the current policy would never choose." — count-based and curiosity bonuses reward states that are _unvisited_ (low $N$, high prediction error); they do not specifically reward states the current policy is unlikely to pick, which is a different quantity (policy uncertainty / state entropy). The intended point — that a novelty bonus is what makes the agent step away from the greedy policy in a deceptive maze — is better made with "reward states the greedy policy would never reach" or by naming the state-entropy objective.

4. `intrinsic-motivation-exploration.md` — "Agent57 is evaluated by per-game success coverage, not by a single averaged score." — Agent57's headline metric is the _median_ human-normalized score over the 57 games (with per-game scores in the tables); "success coverage" is not the term used in the paper and suggests a fraction of games solved, which Agent57 does not report. Suggest "Agent57 reports per-game human-normalized scores and summarizes them by the median across the 57 games".

5. `marl.md` (§4.3, new) — "During training it sees the global state and every agent's action" — that is MADDPG's critic $Q_i(o_1,a_1,\dots,o_n,a_n)$; the section immediately above it describes MAPPO, whose centralized critic is $V_\phi(s)$ and never sees any action. Suggest "the global state (and, in MADDPG, every agent's action)".

6. `review.md` (Exercise 6(b), solution) — "MADDPG's Critic $Q_i(o_1, a_1, \ldots, o_n, a_n)$ conditions on the joint action, restoring a stationary regression target (the remaining drift is only in the _data distribution_ over others' actions, handled by replay of recent joint behavior)." — what becomes stationary is the _environment model_ $P(s'\mid s,a_{1:n})$; the optimal joint value still moves, because the value of a joint action depends on the future behaviour of the other agents, whose policies keep changing. So the critic's regression target is still chasing a moving optimum (this is exactly why MARL needs off-policy data and stabilizers). Suggest "…makes the transition model stationary given the joint action; the target value still drifts with the other agents' policies, which is why MARL uses replay of recent joint behaviour and off-policy critics."

7. `hierarchical.md` (HIRO details block) — "it samples a small set of **candidate goals** — the originally commanded goal $g_t$ plus several goals centered on the actually achieved displacement $s_{t+c} - s_t$" and "$g' \approx \arg\max_{g \in \text{candidates}} \sum_{k=0}^{c-1} \log \pi^{\text{low}}(a_{t+k} \mid s_{t+k}, g)$" — the substance of the correction is right (HIRO maximizes the low-level log-likelihood of the executed actions over a candidate set; it does not simply set $g'=s_{t+c}-s_t$), but two details are uncertain: (i) the paper describes the candidate set as goals sampled around the achieved displacement (the original $g_t$ being one candidate is not something I can confirm), and (ii) the argmax is taken under the _current_ low-level parameters $\phi'$, which the old formula carried ($\pi^{\text{low}}_{\phi'}$) and the new one drops — the "current low-level policy" wording in the prose carries the meaning, but the symbol should keep the prime.

8. `hierarchical.md` (§2 intro vs. §2.3 order) — "Before the three classic methods, we need one shared building block — goal-conditioned policies and hindsight relabeling — and then we look at three classic works" — the building block is actually presented _after_ Options (§2.1) and FeUdal (§2.2). Either move §2.3 up or soften the intro sentence ("Every method below commands its low level with some notion of 'goal', so let's fix that vocabulary first").

9. `hierarchical.md` (§3.1) — "Genie went a step further and introduced **latent action** learning" — the fix (latent actions come from Genie / Bruce et al. 2024, not Genie 3) is correct. But the paragraph it sits in already describes "Genie (Bruce et al. 2024)", so "went a step further" now reads as if Genie followed itself, and after the edit Genie 3 — the real-time world model that the pre-revision sentence was pointing at — is no longer mentioned anywhere in §3.1 or in the chapter's Further Reading. Suggest reworking the paragraph so that Genie (2024) is credited with latent action learning and Genie 3 with the real-time, higher-fidelity world model (or drop the "went a step further" framing entirely).

10. `intrinsic-motivation-exploration.md` (new taxonomy paragraph) — "Eligibility traces, GAE, and Retrace-style multi-step returns propagate the signal backward" — eligibility traces are never taught anywhere in the book (they appear only in `docs/appendix_c_resources/learning-resources.md`), while GAE is introduced in Chapter 8 (`docs/chapter08_ppo/gae-reward-model.md`) and Retrace two sections below. Suggest linking GAE to Chapter 8 and dropping eligibility traces (or pointing at the appendix), so the sentence does not introduce an uncovered term.

## Verdict

This is a careful, technically literate revision: the four suspected errors really were errors (the $10^{-18}$ number was underivable, ICM on Mario is discrete-action, HIRO does pick $g'$ by maximizing the low-level action log-likelihood over candidates, latent actions come from Genie not Genie 3), the PPO chapter reference and the MAPPO min-of-means bug are fixed, the $10^{-39}$/$(1/6)^{50}$ arithmetic, the discount figures, the NGU example, the $6^{10}$ count and both code exercises all check out when recomputed or re-run (plain $-39.0$, bonus $-42.4$; scores $[-25.405,-1.805,-10.205]$, best $4.0$ — exactly as reported). The remaining problems are small but real: one new numeric comparison is off by 14 orders of magnitude (atoms per attempt), one worked exercise solution mis-times the first discounted payment ($5.26$ instead of $4.74$), the new count-bonus formula reuses $\beta$ in a way that double-counts it against the page's own total-reward equation, the ICM/RND sketches remain un-instantiable (`super().__init__()` missing — verified `AttributeError`), and two required repository steps were skipped (the new `review.md` is not in the VitePress sidebar, and `planning/content-review-notes.md` still lists the fixed items as open). Fixing items 1–6 and tightening the handful of over-stated claims in the Doubts section would make this revision mergeable.
