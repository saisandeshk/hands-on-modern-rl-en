# Review of ch25-text

## Errors

1. `docs/chapter25_alignment_failures/rl-evaluation.md` — "$\operatorname{IQM} = \frac{1}{\lfloor M/2 \rfloor}\sum_{i=\lfloor M/4 \rfloor + 1}^{M - \lfloor M/4 \rfloor} s_i$" — The closed form is correct only when $M \equiv 0 \pmod 4$. The number of terms inside the sum is $M - 2\lfloor M/4\rfloor$, and that equals the divisor $\lfloor M/2\rfloor$ only in that case:
   - $M=8$: terms $s_3 \dots s_6$ (4 terms) divided by 4 — correct, and the only case the page illustrates.
   - $M=5$: $\lfloor 5/4\rfloor=1$, $\lfloor 5/2\rfloor=2$ → $(s_2+s_3+s_4)/2$ — three terms divided by two.
   - $M=6$: $\lfloor 6/4\rfloor=1$, $\lfloor 6/2\rfloor=3$ → $(s_2+s_3+s_4+s_5)/3$ — four terms divided by three.
   - $M=3$: $\lfloor 3/4\rfloor=0$, $\lfloor 3/2\rfloor=1$ → $(s_1+s_2+s_3)/1$, i.e. the _sum_ of all three scores, where the IQM should be the median $s_2$.
     The same paragraph recommends "at least 3, ideally 5-10" seeds, so the formula mis-computes for most of the seed counts the section itself suggests.
     — Suggested fix: state the estimator as "discard the lowest $\lfloor M/4\rfloor$ and the highest $\lfloor M/4\rfloor$ scores and average the remaining $M-2\lfloor M/4\rfloor$", or give the quantile form used with the Agarwal et al. paper (mean of $\operatorname{quantile}(s, (k+0.5)/M)$) and keep "the middle 4 of 8" only as the worked example.

2. `docs/chapter25_alignment_failures/rl-evaluation.md` — "Averaging raw scores across tasks is fragile: one saturated task (99-100% for every run) or one broken task can dominate the mean." — The stated mechanism does not hold. A task that scores 99-100 % on every run has zero across-run variance, and $\operatorname{Var}(\bar s)=\frac{1}{T^2}\sum_j s_j^2$ shows that such a task contributes nothing to the variance that the IQM and the stratified bootstrap are meant to control; it only shifts the level of the mean by $1/T$ (1.25 % for 80 tasks), which is not "dominating" it. What actually inflates the uncertainty of the mean is the opposite kind of task — one with high across-run variance. The chapter's own new Exercise 9 gives the correct reasoning for the neighbouring point ("the interval would reflect varying task mixtures rather than genuine score uncertainty"), so the page and the review page disagree.
   — Suggested fix: "the mean is dominated by a few tasks with high across-run variance, and one lucky seed moves it visibly; the IQM trims both tails of the run-score distribution."

3. `docs/chapter25_alignment_failures/rl-evaluation.md` — "Stratification matters because plain resampling across unbalanced groups (say 80 easy and 20 hard tasks) lets the majority group dominate every resample." — This does not distinguish pooled from stratified resampling: with 60 easy and 20 hard tasks the majority group is ~75 % of a pooled resample too. The actual effect of pooling is that the _composition fluctuates_ between resamples (the easy share is $\mathrm{Binomial}(80, 0.75)$, sd $\approx 4.8$ %), which injects task-mixture variance into the interval; stratification fixes the composition and removes that variance. The example is also inconsistent: §25.5.1 uses "80 easy and 20 hard", while `review.md` Exercise 9 uses 60 easy / 20 hard and then refers back to "the same majority-group domination warned about in Section 25.5.1".
   — Suggested fix: give the mixture-fluctuation reason (as Exercise 9 already does) and use one consistent 60/20 example in both places.

4. `docs/chapter25_alignment_failures/rl-evaluation.md` — `return {k: float(np.mean(v)) if v else 0.0 for k, v in results.items()}` — The new empty-group guard turns "this difficulty band contains no problems" into "accuracy 0 % in this band", which is exactly the confusion the section warns about a few lines above ("a reader cannot see that the success rate has already dropped to 0"). With the stub `classify_difficulty` returning `"medium"`, the printed function always reports `easy = 0.0` and `hard = 0.0`, so the sketch can never demonstrate the stratified report it is illustrating. Before the change `np.mean([])` returned `nan`, which was at least unambiguous.
   — Suggested fix: omit empty bands from the returned dict (or return `None` plus a per-band count), and make the `classify_difficulty` stub return different bands so the three buckets are exercised.

## Doubts

1. The IQM used in §25.5.1 is not the canonical estimator. The `rliable` implementation shipped with the Agarwal et al. paper computes the mean of the quantiles at positions $(k+0.5)/M$, not a strict truncation. For the page's own data `[0.42, 0.45, 0.47, 0.48, 0.50, 0.52, 0.55, 0.91]` I computed mean = 0.5375, the page's IQM = 0.4925, and the `rliable`-style IQM = 0.5188 (the top quantile at 0.9375 already reaches deep into the 0.91 outlier). Under the canonical estimator the mean-to-IQM gap is much smaller than the page implies, so a reader who reproduces with the standard library will not obtain the page's numbers. The same holds for Exercise 5's data (mean 0.5125, page IQM 0.475, `rliable`-style 0.4961).

2. §25.2.3 (new): "hand-written shortcuts (hard-coded answers, **empty reasoning with a correct final number**, deleted or skipped tests) that must score 0". In outcome-verified RLVR — the framework this chapter is about — the reward is the final answer, so a bare correct answer legitimately scores 1; requiring "no reasoning" to score 0 is a task policy (e.g. the task demands a derivation, or a process reward is used), not a soundness property of the verifier. As written the bullet prescribes a rule that contradicts the chapter's own RLVR setup.

3. The "Claude Opus 4.6" system card and its four figures (`34×`, `20.96`, `65.83%`, `0.612`), plus the "later system card ... unfixed CPUs ... rerun on unified hardware" claim, cannot be checked without network access. The report flags them as needing external verification, but they are still shipped as facts and are now surrounded by incoherent "around" hedges instead of a source.

4. Structure: the new `### Multiple Seeds, IQM, and Stratified Bootstrap` is the sixth `###` block under `## 25.5.1 Five Standards for Evaluation`, which explicitly enumerates five standards (verifiability, coverage, difficulty stratification, contamination resistance, statistical rigor). A reader sees a heading promising five standards and six subsections. In addition, the Agarwal et al. paper introduced by the new subsection is not added to the "Further Reading" list at the end of §25.5, while every other paper cited in the chapter is.

5. §25.5.2: the prose says "A 13-gram is a fragment of 13 consecutive tokens" (the GPT-3 paper's tokenizer), but the new `get_ngrams` splits on whitespace, so the code computes 13-_word_-grams. The page even notes that "13 is the setting that study adopted under its tokenization and data conditions", which makes the substitution of a different tokenization in the accompanying code a bit incongruous. The code is fine as a sketch, but the mismatch should be stated in a comment.

## Verdict

The branch is careful and mostly right: all nine exercise solutions recompute cleanly (Ex2 gaps 0.37/0.34, Ex3 $1-0.98^{50}\approx63.6\%$, Ex4 SE 0.049 → 50.4 %–69.6 %, Ex5 mean 0.5125 / IQM 0.475, Ex6 0.004/0.70/$\Delta=0.696$/98.2 %, Ex7 $R'=1.95$), the pre-existing numeric examples are correct ($1-0.99^{10}\approx9.6\%$, $1-0.99^{100}\approx63.4\%$, $990/1000=99\%$, the 84 %→79 % alignment tax, the 78 tests / 22 deleted tests), and the two new prose insertions on judge biases and on verifier soundness/completeness are technically sound and consistent with the surrounding chapter. The serious problems are concentrated in the new seeds/IQM material: the closed-form IQM estimator is wrong for every $M$ that is not a multiple of 4 — including most of the seed counts the same paragraph recommends — and both justifications given for IQM and for stratification state the wrong mechanism and contradict the chapter's own Exercise 9. The new review page is also not wired into the sidebar and carries a non-standard "25.R" number that the heading-sync tool cannot normalise, and its Exercise 9 code raises `TypeError` on the natural list inputs. Three citation-level problems survive in the chapter: §25.4.6's debate paper title does not match arXiv 2407.04622, §25.2.1's "RLVR" link points at DeepSeek-R1 (the same URL is cited as DeepSeek-R1 twice elsewhere in the chapter), and the §25.5.7 system-card figures remain high-precision but unverifiable, now behind incoherent "around" hedges that remove the attribution which made them checkable.
