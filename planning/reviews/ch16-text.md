# Review of ch16-text

## Errors

1. `docs/chapter16_reasoning/r1-zero-pure-rl-reasoning.md` — "First, **hold out hidden tests**: grade training rollouts on one set of tests and evaluate on a disjoint, undisclosed set, so memorizing or gaming the visible tests earns no reward." — The causal claim is wrong. In the setup the sentence itself describes, the _training_ reward is computed from the visible tests, so a rollout that memorizes or games those visible tests **does** earn reward during training (that is precisely how test-overfitting reward hacking happens). The hidden set does not change the training reward at all; it only makes the _evaluation_ an unbiased estimate of generalization and therefore the instrument that _reveals_ the overfitting. Hidden tests detect reward hacking, they do not prevent it. — Suggested fix: "First, **hold out hidden tests**: grade training rollouts on the public tests, and evaluate on a disjoint, undisclosed set. The hidden set does not change the training reward, but it makes the evaluation an unbiased estimate and exposes models that have overfit to (or gamed) the visible tests."

2. `docs/chapter16_reasoning/hybrid-thinking.md` — "**On-policy (rollout) distillation**: the student first generates its own attempts (**rollouts**); only the rollouts that pass the verifier are kept for training." plus "The standard training loss is sequence-level cross-entropy against the kept traces" — This contradicts the book's own definition of on-policy distillation in Chapter 15, §15.7 (`docs/chapter15_grpo/on-policy-distillation.md`), which states: "OPD — On-Policy Distillation. The student generates its own rollouts, and an **independent external teacher model** scores each token", with a token-level reverse-KL / top-k KL objective and, explicitly, a "Relationship to SFT / SeqKD" section saying SFT is a _different_ signal. Verifier-filtered self-generated rollouts trained with sequence-level cross-entropy is rejection-sampling fine-tuning (RFT/self-distillation), not OPD: there is no teacher and no token-level teacher signal in the new box. The new box therefore re-defines a Chapter-15 term with a different meaning while sitting two chapters away from the original, and the loss it calls "standard" is the plain SFT/SeqKD negative log-likelihood. — Suggested fix: either delete the box and replace it with a pointer to §15.7, or restate it consistently with §15.7 (student rollouts scored token-by-token by a teacher, reverse-KL token loss) and label the sequence-level cross-entropy as the _off-policy / SFT_ objective it actually is.

3. `docs/chapter16_reasoning/hybrid-thinking.md` — "Suppose the teacher solves 100 problems and the student trains on all 100 traces, of which 30 end in a wrong answer." — Self-contradictory: if the teacher _solves_ the 100 problems, none of its traces can end in a wrong answer. The 30 wrong traces are exactly the ones the verifier would reject, which is the point of the example, but as written the premise and the count cannot both hold. — Suggested fix: "Suppose the teacher generates 100 traces for 100 problems, of which 30 end in a wrong answer."

4. `docs/chapter16_reasoning/hybrid-thinking.md` — "(The sandbox tooling is developed with the [code-generation setup in Chapter 15](../chapter15_grpo/verl-code-sandbox)" — Misattributes the sandbox tooling. A subprocess isolates interpreter state only; **it does not replace a security sandbox**. Run the training job inside a least-privilege container or virtual machine. The complete industrial-level code agent experiment is placed in 19.8." So 15.8 provides a subprocess reward pipeline, not sandbox tooling; the sandbox material is in 19.8. — Suggested fix: "the subprocess-isolated reward pipeline is wired up in the [code-generation setup in Chapter 15](../chapter15_grpo/verl-code-sandbox); a real sandbox (permission checks, resource limits) is covered in [Chapter 19](../chapter19_agentic/overview)."

## Doubts

1. `docs/chapter16_reasoning/emergence-and-o1.md` — "GPT-4o: about 12%" (AIME 2024, single answer) — The page cites the o1 release notes/system card as its source, and in that table OpenAI reports GPT-4o at **9.3 %** pass@1 and **13.3 %** consensus@64 on AIME 2024 (o1-preview: 56.7 % / 61.6 %; o1: 74.4 % / 83.3 %). "About 12 %" sits between the two and does not match the cited source; it is also the number the coverage example `1-(1-0.12)^64 ≈ 99.97 %` is built on (with p = 0.093 the same expression gives ≈ 99.8 %, so the qualitative conclusion survives). Worth checking whether the 12 % comes from a different evaluation (e.g. a third-party pass@1 run) and, if not, replacing it with 9.3 %.

2. `docs/chapter16_reasoning/emergence-and-o1.md` — "o1: 1673" and "The Codeforces Elo of 1673 places o1 at the 89th percentile of the human contestants evaluated." — The pairing looks off: the o1 system card reports o1's Codeforces Elo as **1891** at the 89th percentile (o1-preview: 1188), and 1673 does not appear in the o1 release material I can recall. Please re-check the Elo value against the cited release notes (the percentile figure, 89 %, is the one I would keep).

3. `docs/chapter16_reasoning/adaptive-thinking.md` — "this is the problem that [OpenAI's Instruction Hierarchy](https://openai.com/index/the-instruction-hierarchy/) (2025) addresses" — The Instruction Hierarchy work is from **2024** (arXiv:2404.13273, April 2024; OpenAI's blog post on it is dated 2024 as well). The 2025 date may come from the later "deliberative alignment" material. Please verify.

4. `docs/chapter16_reasoning/hybrid-thinking.md` — "Shortest rejection sampling above is off-policy in this sense: the data is fixed before student training starts." — The criterion stated one clause earlier is "independently of what the student itself would output"; "the data is fixed before student training starts" does not establish that (data produced by the student's own policy and then frozen is still on-policy). It also depends on the Kimi k1.5 implementation: if the traces for shortest rejection sampling are generated by the long-CoT/merged model that is itself being compressed, the "teacher" and the "student" are the same weights, and calling it off-policy distillation is at best ambiguous. Worth pinning down what Kimi k1.5 actually does before keeping the label.

5. `docs/chapter16_reasoning/r1-zero-pure-rl-reasoning.md` — "In real training the weight of the correctness reward is usually much larger than that of the format reward" — In the R1-Zero paper this sentence is about, the two weights are correctness = 1.0 and format = 0.5 (i.e. a factor of 2), not "much larger". The qualitative point (correctness dominates) survives; consider matching the actual ratio.

6. `docs/chapter16_reasoning/adaptive-thinking.md` — "In 2026 Anthropic published a new [Claude Constitution](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-new-constitution)" — I cannot confirm this document, its date, or the URL from memory (the well-known Constitution is from January 2023). The writer already flagged this as unverifiable offline; it should be checked against the live URL before publication, since the whole subsection is built on it.

7. `docs/chapter16_reasoning/hybrid-thinking.md` — "The Kimi k1.5 report says its long2short RL model reaches a Pass@1 of 60.8 on AIME 2024 using only 3,272 tokens on average." — Plausible (it matches the long2short RL operating point I recall from the Kimi k1.5 report), but the writer could not confirm it offline; worth one check against arXiv:2501.12599 since the number is quoted precisely.

## Verdict

The arithmetic in this revision is solid: I recomputed every touched number (`1-0.94^8 = 0.3904`, `0.2·0.8^16 = 0.56 %` for the 17th candidate, the full coverage/marginal-gain table, `p^G+(1-p)^G` for G = 8, all three GRPO advantage examples, the Kimi length rewards, and all eight `review.md` exercise solutions including the NumPy assertions, which all pass). The two items listed in `planning/content-review-notes.md` for Chapter 16 were genuinely wrong and are now genuinely fixed, and the `:::output Output` → `:::output` cleanup is harmless (the custom container renders the same title either way). The problems are concentrated in the two new content blocks: the hidden-test paragraph in 16.2 states a mechanism backwards (hidden tests detect reward hacking, they do not prevent it), and the new distillation box in 16.4 re-defines "on-policy distillation" in a way that contradicts Chapter 15's own definition and mislabels an SFT loss as the OPD objective — that is the most serious finding, since it teaches students the wrong definition two chapters away from the right one. On the process side, the new `review.md` is not in the sidebar (against AGENTS.md and against the writer's own report), uses a `16.R` heading that the heading-fixer can never sync, and the two fixed errors were never recorded in `planning/content-review-notes.md`. Fixing the two new blocks, the sidebar entry/numbering, and the notes file would make this branch ready to merge.
