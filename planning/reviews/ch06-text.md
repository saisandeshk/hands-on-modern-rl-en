# Review of ch06-text

## Errors

1. `docs/chapter06_policy_gradient/review.md` (Exercise 6) — "Show that replacing the full-episode return $G_0$ with the reward-to-go $G_t$ in the step-$t$ gradient term does not change its expectation." — The statement is false under the chapter's own definition of $G_t$ ($G_t=\sum_{k\ge t}\gamma^{k-t}r_k$, as implemented in `compute_returns`), and the supplied solution contradicts it. Decompose $G_0 = \sum_{k<t}\gamma^k r_k + \gamma^t G_t$. Conditioning on the history up to $s_t$, the past-reward part has zero expectation (correct part of the solution), so $\mathbb{E}[\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)G_0] = \gamma^t\,\mathbb{E}[\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)G_t]$. For the chapter's own example ($t=2$, $\gamma=0.9$) the step-2 expectation is $0.81\times$ the reward-to-go expectation, not equal to it; equality would require absolute discounting ($G_t^{\text{abs}}=\sum_{k\ge t}\gamma^k r_k$), which is not what the chapter uses. The solution's own last sentence ("only the $\gamma^t G_t$ part (a positive rescaling of the reward-to-go term) survives") states exactly the factor that refutes the exercise premise. — Suggested fix: restate the exercise as "show that the step-$t$ term's expectation is multiplied by $\gamma^t$ when $G_0$ is used, i.e. the direction is unchanged while the past-reward noise is removed", or use $\gamma^tG_t$ (absolute discounting) in the exercise so the expectations really are identical.

2. `docs/chapter06_policy_gradient/review.md` (Exercise 8 solution) — "with `beta = 0.1` the bonus dominates the return signal and the policy stays near-uniform, so the last-50 average drops markedly (often back toward random-level balancing)" — Off by two to three orders of magnitude. In `reinforce_cartpole.py` the return term is `-(log_probs * returns_tensor).mean()`; per step its logit-gradient magnitude is $(1-p)G_t$, and the mean of $G_t$ over a 100-step CartPole episode with $\gamma=0.99$ is $\frac1{100}\sum_{t}\frac{1-0.99^t}{0.01}=36.6$, so the return gradient is $\approx 0.5\times36.6=18.3$ per step. The entropy gradient is $\beta\,\bigl|p(1-p)\ln\frac{1-p}{p}\bigr|$, whose maximum over $p\in(0,1)$ is $0.2239$ (at $p\approx0.176$). With $\beta=0.1$ that is $0.0224$, i.e. $\approx0.12\%$ of the return gradient; $\beta\approx70$–$100$ would be needed for the two terms to be comparable. At $\beta=0.1$ the policy therefore behaves essentially like vanilla REINFORCE and cannot "stay near-uniform". — Suggested fix: keep the qualitative contrast but state the real scale (in this raw-return loss $\beta$ must be $O(10)$ to matter; $\beta=10^{-3}\dots10^{-2}$ only matters for return-normalized losses as in PPO/RLHF), and replace the predicted outcome with "no measurable difference at $\beta\le0.1$".

3. `docs/chapter06_policy_gradient/pg-improvements.md` (new entropy section) — "With $\beta = 0.01$, the entropy bonus differs by only $0.01 \times (0.693 - 0.325) \approx 0.0037$ per step: a gentle pull that matters most when returns are ambiguous ... In practice $\beta$ is kept small ($10^{-3}$ to $10^{-2}$)" — The paragraph says it "makes the scale clear", but it only compares two entropies and never against the return term of the same loss. In this chapter's own loss the return term per step is $|G_t|\cdot|\log\pi|\approx36.6\times0.69\approx25$, while the whole entropy bonus at $\beta=0.01$ is $0.0069$ nats — three orders of magnitude smaller, so it cannot act as a "pull" here; the $\beta$ range quoted is a PPO/RLHF convention that does not transfer to the raw-return loss used in Section 6.4/6.6. This is the same scale confusion that produces the wrong prediction in `review.md` Exercise 8. — Suggested fix: state the comparison against the return scale (or say explicitly that with unnormalized returns $\beta$ must be $O(10)$, while the $10^{-3}\dots10^{-2}$ range refers to the normalized losses of Chapter 8/13).

4. `docs/chapter06_policy_gradient/reinforce.md` (new "Reward-to-Go" subsection) — "the full-episode return gives a weight of $5.23$ ... The reward-to-go gives the correct, smaller weight $G_2 = 3$, contributing $[0.3, 0.6]$. Same expected direction, less exaggeration from rewards the action did not cause." — The 5.23 vs 3 comparison mixes two different effects. Exactly, $G_0 = (r_0+\gamma r_1) + \gamma^2G_2 = 2.8 + 0.81\times3 = 5.23$: the part attributable to rewards the action did not cause is $2.8$, and the discounted reward-to-go part is $2.43$, not $3$. Using $G_2=3$ also drops the $\gamma^t$ factor, which changes the magnitude of that step's update by $1/0.81\approx23\%$ on top of removing the noise. The arithmetic $[0.1,0.2]\times5.23=[0.523,1.046]$ and $[0.1,0.2]\times3=[0.3,0.6]$ is right, but the conclusion drawn from it is not: the reward-to-go weight that removes _only_ the past rewards is $\gamma^2G_2=2.43$, giving $[0.243,0.486]$. — Suggested fix: compare $5.23$ with $2.43$ (or spell out the two effects separately: $2.8$ of past rewards and the $\gamma^t$ rescaling).

5. `docs/chapter06_policy_gradient/reinforce.md` and `docs/chapter06_policy_gradient/pg-necessity.md` — "Chapter 2 introduced the [policy objective](../chapter02_rl_basics/policy-value) $J(\theta)$" / "the [policy objective $J(\theta)$](./reinforce) introduced in Chapter 2 and derived in Section 6.1" — The notation $J(\theta)$ does not exist in Chapters 2–5: a grep over `docs/` finds $J(\theta)$ only in Chapter 6, Chapter 7/8/9 onward and Appendix D, and `docs/chapter02_rl_basics/policy-value.md` never writes it (it introduces $\pi$, $G_t$, $V^\pi$, $Q^\pi$). ## Doubts

1. `docs/chapter06_policy_gradient/cartpole-baseline.md` (§6.6.4) and the new summary bullet — "the gradient-estimator variance is about `100.41` for vanilla REINFORCE and about `38.27` for the Value Baseline version. That is roughly `38.1%` of the original" (the ratio 38.27/100.41 = 0.3811 is arithmetically correct) — but the shipped code does not measure gradient variance: `reinforce_with_baseline.py` records `grad_estimate = (log_probs * returns_t).mean().item()`, a scalar proxy for the update signal, not a parameter-gradient vector. Its scale differs between the two variants precisely because advantages are smaller than returns, so the 38% ratio mixes a genuine variance-reduction effect with a pure scale effect and does not by itself show that "updates are more consistent". Additionally the two runs use different seeds (0 vs 100), so seed effects are confounded with the method difference. The new summary bullet ("cut gradient-estimator variance to roughly 38% of the vanilla version") repeats the characterization. Not sure how much of this is the code owner's design vs. the text, but the text should not call this quantity "gradient-estimator variance".

2. `docs/chapter06_policy_gradient/pg-improvements.md` — the section heading "The Best Baseline Is $V(s)$" now contradicts the softened body ("it is not exactly the minimum-variance baseline in general"); the heading should be softened too (e.g. "The Standard Baseline Is $V(s)$"). Relatedly, "Baselines reduce variance without changing what the policy converges to" holds only for the expected gradient: with a learned, moving baseline the update path (and the effective step sizes) change, so the claim is true in the limit of exact expectations, not for the algorithm as run.

3. `docs/chapter06_policy_gradient/pg-improvements.md` — "**Chapter 13 RLHF**: a learned reward model supplies the reward signal from which advantage-like comparisons between responses are computed" — In Chapter 13 (PPO-RLHF) the advantage comes from GAE over token-level rewards _within_ one response (`docs/chapter13_rlhf/ppo-rlhf-loop.md`); comparing _responses_ is GRPO's group-relative advantage (Chapter 15). Better: "the reward model's score enters the token-level reward signal; advantages are then estimated over token positions".

4. `docs/chapter06_policy_gradient/cartpole.md` (new Section Summary) — "the next step is to subtract a state-dependent baseline, covered in [6.2 Policy Gradient Improvements](./pg-improvements)" — 6.2 precedes 6.4 and is skipped by this page's own learning path (6.1 → 6.3 → 6.4); the page's §6.4.5 says "the next section runs the value-baseline CartPole experiment" (= 6.5). The summary should point forward to 6.5 and back to 6.2 separately. Also "the same three steps as the bandit" is loose: the bandit has no discounted return computation at all.

5. `docs/chapter06_policy_gradient/review.md` (Exercise 7 solution) — "Typical outcome: both versions finish with $P(B)$ in the $0.85$–$0.95$ band, but the baseline version shows a visibly smaller standard deviation of $P(B)$ over the last 50 episodes" — no run was performed (the report states that no code was executed), and the mechanism offered ("wins on the worse arm no longer push as hard") does not by itself imply smaller fluctuation of $P(B)$: with $r\in\{0,1\}$ the un-baselined update is one-sided (only a win moves the policy), whereas the baselined update moves the policy in both directions with asymmetric magnitudes ($1-b$ up, $-b$ down). A crude estimate in the converged regime ($p(B)=0.9$, $b\approx0.7$) gives per-episode update std $\approx0.48$ (baseline) vs $\approx0.81$ (vanilla), so the claim is plausible but unverified; it should be phrased as "to be measured", not as a result.

6. `docs/chapter06_policy_gradient/review.md` (Exercise 8 solution) — "showing fewer full collapses across seeds" — the exercise as posed trains a single seed ("at the same seed"), so a multi-seed conclusion cannot come out of the described procedure; either the exercise should ask for several seeds or the claim should be dropped.

## Verdict

The revision is mostly careful: the chapter-number corrections, the removed duplicate footnotes, the fixed GIF output path, the added conditioning clause to the baseline proof, the softened Greensmith claim, and the two new section summaries are all correct and verifiable, and every arithmetic example I re-checked (5.23, 4.7, [0.929, 1.957], 6.282, 0.325/0.693/0.500 nats, 2.62, 2.773, 38.27/100.41 = 38.1%, 10^6 s ≈ 11.6 days) is right. The weak points are the two genuinely new technical treatments. The reward-to-go subsection and Exercise 6 rest on a false premise: with the chapter's own relative-discount definition of $G_t$, replacing $G_0$ by $G_t$ changes the step-$t$ expectation by a factor $\gamma^t$ (0.81 in the running example), and the 5.23-versus-3 comparison silently also removes the discount, so the "correct" weight that removes only past rewards is 2.43. The entropy treatment has a scale error of two to three orders of magnitude: in this chapter's raw-return loss a $\beta$ of 0.1 or even 10 is negligible against $G_t\approx37$, so Exercise 8's prediction that $\beta=0.1$ makes the policy "stay near-uniform" cannot happen. Two process problems also remain: the new `review.md` page is not registered in the sidebar (the writer flagged it as pending but did not do it), and the "Chapter 2 introduced $J(\theta)$" cross-reference is still wrong because $J(\theta)$ appears nowhere before Chapter 6.
