## Errors

1. `docs/appendix_a_engineering/training-debugging.md` (A.1, "Watch Three Metrics" → "Metric — KL divergence", ~line 243) —
   "Two common unbiased or low-bias estimators are $k_1 = r$ averaged with the exact term, and Schulman's $k_3 = e^{-r} - 1 + r$, which stays non-negative and is numerically stable when the policies are close." —
   This sentence mixes two incompatible definitions of `r` and gets the estimators wrong for the PPO setting it describes.
   (a) The page defines $r = \log \pi_{\text{new}} - \log \pi_{\text{old}}$ (a *log*-ratio), but Schulman's $k_1$/$k_3$ are defined with the *probability* ratio $\rho = \pi_{\text{new}}/\pi_{\text{old}}$: $k_1 = E[\rho-1]$, $k_2 = E[-\log\rho]$, $k_3 = E[\rho-1-\log\rho]$. Written with the page's own $r$, "$k_1 = r$" has expectation $E_{\pi_{\text{old}}}[r] = -KL(\pi_{\text{old}}\|\pi_{\text{new}})$ (numerically: $\pi_{\text{old}}=(0.5,0.5)$, $\pi_{\text{new}}=(0.7,0.3)$ gives $E[r] = -0.0872$), i.e. the **negative** of the KL the reader is supposed to monitor, and "averaged with the exact term" is not a formula at all. The unbiased estimate of $KL(\pi_{\text{old}}\|\pi_{\text{new}})$ from old-policy rollouts is $-E[r] = E[\log\pi_{\text{old}}-\log\pi_{\text{new}}]$, which is exactly what `approx_kl` logs in TRL/veRL.
   (b) $k_3 = e^{-r}-1+r$ uses the **inverted** ratio. For rollouts sampled from $\pi_{\text{old}}$ it is *not* an unbiased estimate of $KL(\pi_{\text{old}}\|\pi_{\text{new}})$: with the same toy pair it returns 0.10330 versus the true 0.08718 (+18.5 %, biased upward by the Rényi-2 excess $\sum_x \pi_{\text{old}}^2/\pi_{\text{new}}$). The unbiased, always-non-negative form for old-policy samples is $e^{r}-1-r$ with $r=\log(\pi_{\text{new}}/\pi_{\text{old}})$, which returns exactly 0.08718 — and that is what the repository's own PPO code computes: `code/chapter01_cartpole/2-pytorch_ppo.py:262-264` uses `log_ratio = new_log_probs - batch_old_log_probs` and `((log_ratio.exp() - 1) - log_ratio).mean()`.
   The page therefore contradicts both its own repo code and the book's own reference on this topic, `docs/appendix_b_algorithms/sft-kl.md` (B.1), which defines $k_3$ with $\Delta = \log(q/p)$, samples from $p$, and warns: "In k3 the ratio must be $q/p$ (ref/current) … flipping it to $p/q$ keeps the value nonnegative but the expectation is no longer $KL(p\|q)$" (also "k3 ratio reversed — Must be $q/p$; flipping it biases the expectation").
   Suggested fix: rewrite the bullet with the probability ratio and the sampling direction made explicit, e.g. "with $\rho = \pi_{\text{new}}/\pi_{\text{old}}$ and rollouts drawn from $\pi_{\text{old}}$, the unbiased estimate of $KL(\pi_{\text{old}}\|\pi_{\text{new}})$ is $E[\log\pi_{\text{old}}-\log\pi_{\text{new}}] = -E[\log\rho]$ (this is what `approx_kl` logs), and Schulman's low-variance form is $E[\rho-1-\log\rho] = e^{r}-1-r$, which is non-negative and second-order accurate near $r=0$; the inverted form $e^{-r}-1+r$ is only unbiased when the samples come from $\pi_{\text{new}}$ (the GRPO/reference-anchored case of [B.1](../appendix_b_algorithms/sft-kl))".

## Doubts

1. `docs/appendix_a_engineering/training-debugging.md` (A.1, new section "Report Results with Uncertainty, Not Single Seeds", ~line 449) —
   "Henderson et al. showed that the same algorithm and hyperparameters can produce markedly different results under different random seeds, **and that implementation details alone can flip algorithm comparisons**[^drltm]." The second clause is the canonical finding of Engstrom et al., *Implementation Matters in Deep RL* — which this very page already cites as `[^implementation]`. Henderson et al. supports seed/hyperparameter sensitivity (and does discuss reward scaling), so the sentence is not clearly wrong, but bundling both claims under `[^drltm]` loses the distinction the page draws elsewhere. Suggest splitting the citation: `[^drltm]` for seeds, `[^implementation]` for implementation details.

2. `docs/appendix_a_engineering/training-debugging.md` (A.1, "Metric — clip fraction", ~line 245) —
   "the fraction of tokens or samples where the PPO probability ratio $\pi_{\text{new}} / \pi_{\text{old}}$ **hit the clipping bounds** $[1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon]$". Chapter 1 gives the precise (and different) definition: "The clip fraction counts the share of samples that fall outside this range: $\operatorname{clipfrac}=\frac{1}{|B|}\sum_i \mathbf 1[|r_i(\theta)-1|>\epsilon]$", and the repo code computes `((ratio - 1.0).abs() > clip_eps).float().mean()` (`code/chapter01_cartpole/2-pytorch_ppo.py:265`). "Hit the bounds" can be read as "reached the bound" (= clipped = outside), but it can also be read as "inside/at the interval", which is the opposite. Suggest: "the fraction of samples whose probability ratio fell **outside** $[1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon]$, i.e. whose surrogate objective was clipped" and link Chapter 1.

3. `docs/appendix_a_engineering/evaluation-badcase.md` (A.4, new subsection, report template ~lines 292-300 and the sentence right after) —
   The worked example shows "task_success IQM 46.2% [43.1, 49.0] vs 41.0% [38.2, 43.9]" — the two intervals overlap on [43.1, 43.9] — while the sentence immediately below says "If the confidence intervals overlap substantially … the correct conclusion is 'no evidence yet'". The example therefore does not satisfy the rule the page states for it (it is presented as a template with a conditional decision, so this may be intentional, but the numbers invite the reader to copy a case that fails its own gate). Two related points: (i) the correct test for "did the gap survive" is the CI of the *paired* per-seed difference, not the overlap of two marginal CIs; (ii) with only 5 seeds, a 95 % CI of ±2.95 points implies a seed-to-seed std of ≈2.38 points (half-width $= t_{0.975,4}\,s/\sqrt5 = 1.241\,s$), which is optimistic — rliable's own recommendation is ≥10 runs, which is why "five to ten" and a ±3-point CI sit awkwardly together.

7. `docs/appendix_a_engineering/monitoring.md` ("When You Should Stop", item 1) —
   "**KL divergence exceeds a threshold** (often 0.1-0.15): the policy has drifted too far." The book's own runs report much smaller values — Chapter 1: "The maximum over the 40 iterations in this run is 0.00871"; Chapter 8: approximate KL "below 0.016 most of the time" — and LLM RLHF/GRPO frameworks typically set `target_kl` around 0.001–0.01. A 0.1–0.15 stop threshold would essentially never fire; it may be a KL *coefficient* that was intended. Since the writer already edited this page's lookup table, worth checking.

Consider "the median has higher variance than the IQM at the same sample size".

The code's math is fine (it is the unbiased $k_3$ for old-policy samples); only the attribution to SB3 is off.

## Verdict

The revision is careful and mostly improves the appendix: the two inverted PPO quick-lookup fixes in `monitoring.md` are correct, the memory arithmetic in `parallelism.md` checks out (7B FP16: 14 GB params + 14 GB grads + 56 GB Adam FP32 = 84 GB, i.e. "roughly six times" 14 GB), the removal of the hardware-specific GRPO timing table and of the fabricated "2–4× PagedAttention" / "25 %/50 %/40 % savings" figures is a clear improvement, and the radar-script output-path fix is correct (`docs/appendix_industrial_training/images` does not exist; `docs/appendix_a_engineering/images` does, and both PNGs are referenced). The new uncertainty/CI material in A.1 and A.4 is well sourced (Agarwal et al., NeurIPS 2021) and is a real gap in the book. The most important problem is the new KL-estimator bullet in A.1: it mixes the log-ratio and probability-ratio conventions, calls an estimator "unbiased" that has the wrong sign or an ~18 % bias depending on which of the two is meant, and contradicts both the book's own PPO code and Appendix B.1's explicit warning about flipping the k3 ratio. Second is the arithmetic slip in the A.4 SWE-bench table (+4.4 should be +5.4), followed by the AgentBench radar, where the plotted data, the printed table, and the described "relative-to-best" normalization are three different stories. None of these are hard to fix, but the KL bullet should be corrected before the page teaches a wrong estimator to readers who will then compare it against their own logs.
